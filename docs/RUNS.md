## R9 — answered: do not adopt the second player's double first turn

R9 asked whether letting Black make two moves on its first turn cancels or over-compensates the
first-move edge. **It over-compensates on the pool the game ships with.** `dt-full` takes White from
0.533 to 0.472 — it removes an edge of +0.033 and hands Black one of +0.028, which is a mirror image
and not a correction. `dt-nopal` lands at 0.490, near even, so the size of the over-correction tracks
the paladin. `QUEUE.md` set the bar at "below 0.47 is out"; 0.472 sits on it and the paired interval
(±0.026) puts the whole of it below 0.50. Nothing else in the game moves: draws, decisiveness,
length and branching are all flat in both pools.

The cost is not only the number. Black's first turn stops being a chess turn, and the tilt the rule
corrects is one classical chess has accepted for a century (White scores about 54–55% at master
level). The compensation devices chess practice actually uses are komi-style — the **pie rule** and
**Armageddon** — and both leave the moves alone and put the compensation in the conditions. That is
the family to look at if Saar ever wants an equaliser. **Verdict: do not adopt. `RUNS.md` R9 closed.**

Two rows of both `dt-*` experiment files are invalid: `killerMove` (−0.21) and `interest` (−0.04).
`leadMetrics()` identifies the mover by ply parity, and Black's double turn inverts the parity for the
rest of the game. See `sim-queue-2026-09-14.md` §5.2. Every parity-free metric is sound.

**Qualification (2026-09-16, takeover review §Spec 2).** The defect is not only in the two analysis
rows: the *search itself* assumes alternating movers — it derives the side to move from `rootTurn ^
(ply & 1)`, negates every child score, toggles the turn bit in the Zobrist key and walks repetition
paths by alternating indices. Under this toggle those assumptions are false, so the depth-3 balance
numbers above are directional only and are not trustworthy evidence for the toggle. That does not
reopen the owner's decision: **the rule stays rejected and nothing here is rerun.** A later feature
that needs extra turns (Haste, for example) must first make the search track the actual mover,
correct the score sign, the hash, repetition and exact state serialization. No command from this
section is in the active queue.

## R10 — answered: the Wall stays

Saar rejected the two-square Warden on draw grounds (2026-09-14) and asked for a pawn-style double
step from the home rank instead. Both readings ran (`ab-guard-dbl-slide`, `ab-guard-dbl-leap`) and
both drag drawn games as hard as the Warden did: a guard sits among the side's last three pieces or
beside its own king in **51.5%** and **51.9%** of drawn side-games, against the Wall's **40.5%** and
the rejected Warden A's 52.0%. The argument that a move-one rule cannot touch the endgame does not
survive the measurement. **Verdict: keep the Wall. `guardDoubleFirst` stays a lab toggle.**

## Void run — `pb-ab-base`

**`pb-ab-base` is void as a control for any comparison from 2026-09-14 on. Do not resume it and do not
base a new A/B on it. It stays on disk as the evidence behind the `LESSONS.md` entry.**

It was played 1 544 games under the **two-guard** pool and then resumed for 56 under the one-guard
pool, so its 1 600 games cover **79 arrangements**, of which **384 games hold a two-guard back rank**.
Only **94 of its games** fall on the 40 arrangements today's arms play, so a "paired" A/B against it
pairs almost nothing. Its capped share is **7.1%** against the clean control's 1.0% — read against it,
both buffs of Q2/Q3 looked like cures (capped 7.1% → 0.9%) when neither touches the ply cap at all.
`pb-ab-base24` is the clean control: same 40 arrangements as both arms, one guard per rank, capped 1.0%.

Lesson: `LESSONS.md` — "A resumed control run silently mixes pools" (2026-09-14). After any change to
`POOL` or `DEFAULT_RULES`, give the control a **new id**; before reading an A/B, check that the
control's arrangement set equals the arm's.
