- **Immortality does not hurt the fun.** With one guard per army the cost is small: two guards used to cost about nine decisive games per hundred and 3% stuck games, inside our limits. What drags games out is a guard that can *reach* or *eat pawns*, not one that cannot be captured.
- **How to use an immortal guard well.** The paying pattern is the anvil: park the guard next to an enemy piece so it cannot step away, then take it with an archer shot or a beast. Using the guard as a bodyguard next to the king is what players and the AI do by default, and it scores worse; a guard placed four or more files away from its king makes games about ten points more decisive with no balance cost. Escorting a pawn to promotion with the guard does not work.
- **On the two-square guard, the two studies agree on the data and differ on the judgement.** The Warden test liked it for activity (four times as many moves, more shielding) at the cost of longer games and more guard-caused draws; the guard study weighs those draws as the whole problem and says keep the one-square Wall.
