# King Down showcase judgement

Keep the proposed blend. Change how its parts join.
Quiet Table gives the game a clear centre. Powers and special moves give it its own character.
The best ideas explain a rule through an action. The weakest ideas add a display mode or another setting.

I read the brief and all 27 demo metadata files. I inspect all 54 phone and desktop contact sheets.
I check selected source files against `docs/RULES.md` and `research/king-down-facts.md`.
The findings below refer to named demo states. Motion comments use the metadata and source timings.
Browser approval review refuses the local prototype because permission is denied. Live input, motion and access checks remain unverified.
I work alone. I do not read the other judge's folder.

## 1. Each demo

### `dir-quiet-table` — KEEP

**Best:** The board stays clear in `read`, `check` and `end`. The result sits below or beside it.
**Main issue:** Desktop keeps the move list open. The owner's request to fold the transcript applies there too.
**Fix:** Use the same closed last-move line on both sizes. Open the list only when the player asks.

### `dir-arena` — CHANGE

**Best:** The power reveal explains why the Archer can move like a queen. The spent power keeps a visible record.
**Main issue:** The open tray repeats the last move and makes the history compete with the board.
**Fix:** Use B on both sizes. Keep the named power tile and one brief reveal. Open the tray on demand.

### `dir-pocket` — DROP as a complete direction

**Best:** The bottom actions and landscape rail put controls within thumb reach.
**Main issue:** In phone `read` and `story`, the sheet covers the lower board. The large empty area above it cannot help the player.
**Fix:** Keep the bottom bar in Quiet Table. Put the read card in a fixed area outside the board. Use the loupe only during a drag.

### `dir-chronicle` — CHANGE

**Best:** `previously` restores the cause of the current position. The recap gives special moves a lasting record.
**Main issue:** The closed story still has three rows. The move number, rows and live line use too much of a phone screen.
**Fix:** Keep one last-move sentence at rest. Show the two earlier rows only on return. Put the recap behind Review.

### `dir-coach` — CHANGE

**Best:** `refusal` names the Guard rule at its cause. `retry` gives a loss a useful next action.
**Main issue:** Full help combines badges, danger marks, ghosts and four check choices. This can replace the player's decision with a list of answers.
**Fix:** Show the rule and cause first. Put check choices behind Hint. Keep Retry available at every computer level. Fade help by the rules a player learns.

### `dir-compare` — KEEP as a review tool

**Best:** The same state picker makes the differences easy to compare.
**Main issue:** The pieces, levels and selected actions differ between frames. These changes weaken the comparison.
**Fix:** Use the same level, piece, move and power record in all five frames. Use Two for detailed reading.

### `feat-first-minute` — KEEP

**Best:** A shot over a pawn shows the game's main surprise before a setup form appears.
**Main issue:** The practice shot ends, then the full army starts again. The smooth change can make this look like one continuous game.
**Fix:** Keep A's close view and pull-back. Use one clear line at the change: “Practice complete. Your first game starts.” Start Beginner at once.

### `feat-home` — KEEP

**Best:** `table` and `continued` keep the same board in the same place. Continue has one clear purpose.
**Main issue:** A king scene or Today's army can replace the saved game. A small warning is easy to miss.
**Fix:** Send each new-game action through the same setup sheet. Name the game it replaces. Keep Continue as the only primary action while a game is active.

### `feat-new-game` — CHANGE

**Best:** The closed Side and Army row shows its values. The fixed Start button gives the form a clear end.
**Main issue:** Some short power lines omit a rule that changes the choice. Mercy loses its shelter; Darkness loses its king move.
**Fix:** Keep A and B. Add a readable Rules detail for each power. Put every condition there. Show the other king and power in one closed Change row.

### `feat-muster` — KEEP

**Best:** Each pair enters together. This shows the same-army rule without a paragraph.
**Main issue:** Four tags can appear at once in `first-sight`. The reveal then becomes another tour.
**Fix:** Keep the short flip. Show one name for each unfamiliar type, with no repeated tags. End all entry motion on the first input.

### `feat-hold-to-read` — KEEP

**Best:** `hold-target` lets a player read a legal enemy target without taking it. `short-hold` keeps the actions clear.
**Main issue:** A tap on a marked enemy plays, while another enemy tap reads. A player needs to know this difference before the first take.
**Fix:** Show “Tap to take. Hold to read.” at the first selected target. Keep the I key and a Rules button as visible alternatives.

### `feat-your-move` — CHANGE

**Best:** A shove arrow shows where the other piece lands. “Stop here” makes a Beast chain an explicit choice.
**Main issue:** The numbered second bite appears before the first bite. It can look like a required sequence.
**Fix:** Mark the first legal bites. After a bite, number the bites already made and show the next legal targets. Keep Stop here beside the board.

### `feat-their-turn-check` — KEEP

**Best:** The check arc explains the shot over f2. The checking piece, king mark and word Check agree.
**Main issue:** Three check signals plus sound and a buzz can draw attention away from the cause.
**Fix:** Keep the cause line and one still mark on the king. Keep Check in text. Remove the extra portrait rim. Give vibration its own switch.

### `feat-king-powers` — CHANGE

**Best:** “Freeze · 1 left” and “Then make your move” explain the power and its two steps.
**Main issue:** The three-beat cast takes about 0.8 seconds. A routine mark gets a large share of the turn.
**Fix:** Keep A. Show target, ice and count change in one 250–350 ms action. Let the player make the next move at once.

### `feat-help` — CHANGE

**Best:** Undo names its scope. The rewind shows why a taken piece returns.
**Main issue:** Second look stops play on a rough material check. Its own notes say an existing threat can cause the stop.
**Fix:** Keep A for games and B for lessons. Make second look an optional practice aid. Use the game's evaluation, not a short material count.

### `feat-move-story` — CHANGE

**Best:** One sentence answers what the other side did. The Beast ghost makes an old chain easy to understand.
**Main issue:** A past position plays over current pieces. Two times on one board can become hard to read.
**Fix:** Enter a clear Review state before the replay. Show the exact old position. Keep Back to game visible and disable live moves there.

### `feat-menu-extra` — KEEP

**Best:** Named rows are fast to scan. A found trick gives Extra a reason to exist.
**Main issue:** Menu → Extra → Board help puts useful board controls three steps away.
**Fix:** Put Board help beside Guide in Menu. Keep Workshop, Tricks, Account and game export in Extra. Give each new trick one quiet signal.

### `feat-lessons` — KEEP

**Best:** The Guard lesson teaches movement, blocking, the king exception and a final choice. It does more than show a move.
**Main issue:** The final choice still marks the legal answer. A player can finish without testing the Guard rule.
**Fix:** Make the last board an unmarked choice. Use “Lesson complete” after success. Keep every piece available from the shelf.

### `feat-king-down` — KEEP

**Best:** The fallen king and final cause stay visible. Retry gives the player a way to learn from a loss.
**Main issue:** “Moves to look at again” in the win uses positions from different games. It cannot prove the recap of this game.
**Fix:** Use only the recorded game's positions. Choose moments through the existing analysis. Keep Quiet as the default and final-blow replay on demand.

### `feat-share` — CHANGE

**Best:** The sender sees the exact text or picture before sharing. The move card shows a reason to try King Down.
**Main issue:** The emoji line has no key. A person who does not play cannot read a paw, bolt and ogre face as a result.
**Fix:** Default to the short words and counts. Offer marks as a compact choice. Keep level and mode in every result. Make the dated link open that date's army.

### `feat-joy-dial` — DROP the player dial

**Best:** Three versions of the same moments give the owner a useful way to judge restraint.
**Main issue:** Calm changes sound and event marks as well as motion. A Sound switch can be on while Calm remains silent.
**Fix:** Use Warm as the design target. Keep simple Sound, Vibration and Motion controls. Keep all rule marks at every setting. Put the large final replay behind a button.

### `future-card-hand` — KEEP

**Best:** A folded hand protects the board. Open cards name whether they use the turn or precede a move.
**Main issue:** Six cards can remain hidden behind a count. A new player can miss the main feature of this mode.
**Fix:** Open the hand once on the first card-mode turn. Let the first board action fold it. Later, keep the same quiet Cards row.

### `future-crowns` — CHANGE

**Best:** The paint reveal and immediate piece lesson make the reward useful. The live board has no reward meter.
**Main issue:** The loss screen gives space to a piece the player still cannot earn at Beginner. It directs attention to another win requirement.
**Fix:** Put the loss and Retry first. Fold crown progress after a loss. Keep the full piece lesson open at every level, with the condition in plain words.

### `future-online` — CHANGE

**Best:** The invite names mode, army, powers and first move. Previously shows why the position changes.
**Main issue:** Reconnecting says the move is safe, but no state shows how an unsent move becomes accepted by the other side.
**Fix:** Show Saved here, Ready to send, Link copied, and Accepted online as distinct states. Keep the board usable for reading during recovery.

### `future-workshop-share` — KEEP

**Best:** Change one thing starts from a piece that works. The same card carries the figure, movement and rules to a friend.
**Main issue:** Fair appears as a strong label. The estimate has an error band, but the card hides it.
**Fix:** Use “Estimated worth: about 3 pawns.” Put the range and reasons one tap away. Keep “Test board only” on the card and received view.

### `kit` — CHANGE

**Best:** The demos use the real figures, legal moves and computer player. This gives the design review a sound base.
**Main issue:** The basic target marks do not distinguish all the new verbs. A plain ring cannot explain a shove or a swap.
**Fix:** Put the chosen verb marks and reading shapes in the shared kit. Use them in every demo.

### `proto` — CHANGE

**Best:** The board, two player strips, one context line and one folded Moves line form a clear game screen.
**Main issue:** Undo removes one or two records. A free power plus a move is more than one record. Undo can split that turn.
**Fix:** Undo whole turns, including power marks, both Haste moves and the reply. Restore the power count and target with the board.

## 2. Rank the five directions

`dir-compare` is a review tool. It is not a sixth product direction.

| Rank | Direction | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Quiet Table | Use its light floor, stable board and quiet end as the base. |
| 2 | Arena | Add its named powers and brief opponent reveal in powers and card modes. |
| 3 | Coach | Add rule refusals, reading and Retry. Make deeper help optional. |
| 4 | Chronicle | Add one move sentence, Previously and a recap in Review. |
| 5 | Pocket | Drop the full direction. Reuse its bottom actions, landscape rail and drag loupe. |

This blend borrows the useful parts of card games: identity, visible causes and short reveals.
It does not need a hand tray in every chess game.
Keep one frame for all modes. Add only the controls that the chosen mode needs.
Let a device dark-mode choice change the floor. Keep the board and marks equally readable.

## 4. Does `proto` hold together?

Yes, as a core product design. Its game screen has a clear order: board, current action, last move, tools.
The power tile fits the same frame. The end uses the board rather than a second scene.

**Home is its weakest screen.** It loses the main strength of `feat-home` A.
Continue covers the lower saved board. The desktop home board is much smaller than the game board.
Today's army is a full card, although the lead recommends one folded line.
There is no last-move sentence to restore the game in memory.

Use the feature demo's Home layout directly. Keep the board at its game size and position.
Put Continue below or beside it. Show the last move. Fold Today into one named row.
After a finished game, offer Review as well as Rematch.

The first-shot screen also loses A's close view and pull-back. Restore that moment.
The title shows six kings and twelve pieces before the player takes one shot. Fold the small piece catalogue into Guide.

The blend is less complete than its feature demos:

- The result shows large takes, not the existing key-moment analysis. Keep its cautious label until analysis is connected.
- Retry and found Tricks are absent. Add these before more visual effects. They give the player a reason to return.
- The read card repeats the context line. Use the context for the immediate fact and the card for the diagram and conditions.
- The setup hides the opponent's power and has no No power choice. Bring over `feat-new-game`'s closed opponent row.
- Guide, Workshop, Board help and Account are notes. The report does not treat these notes as completed journeys.

## 5. Too much

- **Stacked history:** Arena's tray and Chronicle's three closed rows add weight at rest. Keep one sentence.
- **Stacked reveals:** A coin flip, named card, trail, cause line, king shake and sound can mark one Strike. Use the power name and cause; then stop.
- **Repeated check motion:** `feat-their-turn-check` removes the flinch, but Warm and `proto` restore it. Keep the king still so its square and threat remain clear.
- **Slow routine powers:** The 0.8-second Freeze cast is longer than the brief's normal 120–400 ms motion range. Shorten it after first use.
- **Repeated entry effects:** Use the full muster for a new army. A rematch and a return to a game need no full deal.
- **Too many settings:** History style, hint style, help level, Joy, motion speed and sound create a second design task for the player. Choose one good default for each task.
- **Hidden core help:** Fold optional destinations. Keep rule help and access controls close to the board.
- **Crowns after a loss:** A reward goal takes space from the rule that caused the loss. Show the lesson first.
- **Fair in Workshop:** A prominent estimate can look like a measured guarantee. Name the estimate and show its range on demand.
- **Inconsistent names:** Use Today's army throughout. `proto` changes it to Today's game in Extra. Use the same piece names and verbs in every screen.

The red movement dots in `proto/game-read` also need a clear meaning.
The Guard card says it never takes, but its empty step squares have red dots.
Use neutral step marks and a small Step key. Reserve threat marks for takes.

## 6. Missing player needs

1. **Complete special-turn journeys.** Show Haste after move one, End turn, Ice Wall then move, and Undo for each. Show a Beast with more than one legal continuation. The current samples concentrate on Freeze and one chain.
2. **Pawn promotion.** No demo shows the four-piece choice or the resulting rule card. Show queen, rook, bishop and knight in a clear picker.
3. **Draw completion.** No contact sheet gives a full draw journey. Show stalemate, repetition and the 50-move reason. Do not topple a king for a draw.
4. **Access through a full task.** Some demos show keyboard reading. None proves start → move → power → review with a screen reader and keyboard. Check 44 px targets, large text, contrast and short browser viewports.
5. **A persistent check state.** `proto` puts reading and selection before check in its context logic. Keep a short Check label visible while a player reads or selects a piece.
6. **A simple guide to the base differences.** No castling, no en passant and standard promotion matter to a chess player. Offer one short “What changes from chess?” page in Guide.
7. **Reliable saved-game recovery.** Show an active game that survives refresh, a failed save, and a choice between two synced versions. A quiet note must say when progress is safe.
8. **The move after reconnection.** Show what remains local, what is sent, and what the other player accepts. A copied link does not prove delivery.
9. **A full friend journey in the blend.** `future-online` has an invite preview. `proto` has only one-device play. Show both link play and one-device handover, including whose pieces the next player controls.

These are requested proof screens. Their absence does not mean the current app lacks each function.

## 7. Delight

**First: the first shot grows into the game.** The Archer stays still while a pawn falls beyond a blocker.
The pull-back turns a small rule lesson into a full world. Keep this as the first memory.

**Second: Undo runs the special move backward.** A shot returns and the taken piece stands again.
The motion makes a useful action clear. Keep it short and make repeated Undo immediate.

**Third: a found trick becomes a seal and a riddle.** This rewards discovery rather than repetition.
A far swap or Guard shove is worth remembering. Keep the signal brief and the collection out of live play.

**Add: turn a found trick into a puzzle for a friend.** Use the recorded position before that move.
Ask “Can you find the two bites?” or “Can you move the Guard?” Hide the answer until the friend tries.
Then play the original move and name its rule. Open this from the found trick or result, not from the live board.
It joins Tricks, lessons and sharing with one small playable object.

## 8. Rule and fact checks

The shown Archer shot over f2 and the mate from f6 fit today's Archer rule.
The Guard refusal, Guard shove, Maester swap and voluntary Stop here also fit the stated rules.
Freeze has one use; Ice Wall has two. Strike moves a non-pawn piece to an empty square and uses the turn.
A frozen Archer still gives check. This agrees with the engine; it is not a demo error.

The following text or state needs correction:

| Place | Fault | Concrete correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `proto`, draw result text | “Fifty moves with no take” omits pawn moves. | Say “50 moves with no take or pawn move.” Use a Draw heading, not the desktop King down heading. |
| `proto`, Darkness | The two-square king step omits the empty middle square. | Add “over an empty square.” State that the destination is empty and the move takes nothing. |
| `feat-new-game`, Darkness | The selected power line omits the king's two-square step entirely. | Include both pawn movement and the king step in Rules detail. |
| `feat-new-game`, Mercy | The line omits the shelter and says “steps two.” | Say one or two squares. Add that only pawns can take pieces next to this king. |
| `proto` and `feat-new-game`, Haste | The line can make two moves sound compulsory. | Say the second move is optional. Keep End turn visible after move one. |
| `proto`, Beast read card | “After each bite, it can bite again” has no limit in its detail. | Add “A later bite in the chain cannot take a king.” |
| `proto`, Holy Light | Its power detail omits that the king may take pawns. | Add this fact beside the pawn immunity and four-square shelter. |
| `proto`, Undo | One or two records can split a power turn. | Return to the start of the player's turn. Restore all counts, marks and moves, then remove the reply. |
| `future-online`, `result` | Metadata says the engine has Strike unused while the plate says used. | Replay the recorded moves and derive the plate from that state. Never maintain a separate power history. |
| `dir-pocket`, `end` | Strike says ready after its use at move 12. | Read the remaining use count from the end position. |
| `feat-king-down`, winning recap | The sampled moments do not form one recorded game. | Use a real complete game. Until then, label these tiles as separate examples. |

Most power issues above are missing conditions in text. I do not find evidence that these screens change the legal-move engine.
A short live line can omit detail if a complete rule is available. The chosen-power view and full read card must give that detail.

Keep the current Archer shot pattern in these demos. The newer far2 choice is not merged into today's game.
Keep the Paladin marked as a bonus or custom piece. It is outside the random pool.
Keep card hands, crown counts and online service states marked as proposals. The six-card deal is not settled.
Rage's Rare label is also a proposed presentation choice. Do not imply that random rarity or card ownership is an adopted rule.
