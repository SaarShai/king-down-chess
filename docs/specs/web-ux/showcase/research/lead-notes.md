# Lead notes: my own point of view

Written before I read the research and the advisors, so that my view stays independent.

## Thesis

The board is the stage. Everything else waits in the wings and steps in only when it has a job. King Down is chess plus magic (new pieces, king powers, later cards). Chess apps show the chess and hide the magic in menus. We show the magic on the board, at the moment it happens, and hide the menus.

## Signature ideas

1. **Quiet chrome.** The game screen is the board, two nameplates (you and the opponent, each with a king portrait) and one action bar: Hint, Undo, Menu. Nothing else at rest.
2. **Move ribbon and story.** Closed: one ribbon under the board with the last move as glyphs (piece icon, verb mark, target). Open: a story feed, one short line per move with icons and verbs ("shoots", "bites twice", "pushes"). A tap on a line replays that move on the board as a ghost. Key moments carry a gold mark. Borrowed from the action logs of Root and Wingspan and from Marvel Snap's turn summary.
3. **Hold to read.** A long press (desktop: hover) lifts the figure and slides up a card: portrait, name, one line on its ability, and a small board that draws its moves in sequence. Works for enemy pieces. Borrowed from the Hearthstone and Marvel Snap card zoom.
4. **First sight.** The first time a new piece type is on your board, it carries a small sparkle and one quiet line: "New: Archer. Hold to read." Once only. Borrowed from Hearthstone's keyword glow and Into the Breach's clarity.
5. **The king is the power button.** In a powers game your king portrait is the power button, with pips for its charges. A tap dims the board except the legal targets. The power resolves with one short signature effect (Freeze: frost creeps over the square). The opponent's power gets a short reveal on its portrait. Borrowed from Hearthstone's hero power and Snap's reveal.
6. **Danger preview.** While you hold a piece over a square, small marks show which enemy pieces can then take it. Default on for Beginner, a switch for the others. Borrowed from Into the Breach.
7. **Check without alarm.** An ember ring on the king, a low drum, a crown glyph in the ribbon. No red flash.
8. **King Down.** At the end the losing king topples, the board fades a little except the winning piece, and the words "King Down" settle in. Then a result card: three key moments, Rematch, Review, Share. Borrowed from Snap's end of match and the Wordle share card.
9. **Home is the table.** A returning player sees the last game's board with Continue on it, today's board as one card, and New game. Nothing more.
10. **Opponents are kings.** The computer levels become six king characters with a temper (Flame attacks, Frost defends). One picker for the level and, in powers games, for the power. Borrowed from fighting game character select and Snap's deck picker.
11. **The cabinet.** Extra is a cabinet of small illustrated tiles, each with a one-line tease: Workshop, Today's board, Kings' powers, Card mode (soon). A new tile carries a gold dot once. Locked pieces show as silhouettes with the way to earn them.
12. **Crowns.** A thin crown track on the result card. An unlock flips the piece's card over, then offers a one-minute lesson.
13. **Feel.** Each piece type has its own soft step sound (the Ogre thuds, the Archer twangs). A light haptic tap on pickup, a firmer one on a capture. All quiet by default.
14. **Rewind, not jump.** Undo plays the move backward.
15. **The opponent thinks.** The opponent's portrait breathes while the computer thinks. No spinner.
16. **Hint in three steps.** First tap: the piece glows. Second: the target appears. Third: one line on why.
17. **Review as a scrubber.** A timeline with a tick for each move and gold marks for key moments. Drag it like a video.
18. **Cards later.** Card mode: a hand that rests as three small cards at the edge and fans open on touch. A card uses the same inspect card as a piece.
19. **Online, kindly.** "Finding a table" with the opponent's king appearing. Four short emotes only (Well played, Wow, Oops, Thanks). No chat by default.
20. **Coordinates on demand.** Hidden at rest; they appear on the edge while a finger is on the board, and in review.

## Directions I expect

- **Quiet Table:** calm and minimal, for players who want only the chess.
- **Arena:** the card-battler stage, subtle: nameplates, hero power, reveals.
- **Chronicle:** the game as a tale; the story feed is the soul of the screen, but it is folded.
- **Pocket:** one hand, thumb zone, sheets and swipes.
- **Coach:** learning first: hints, danger preview and first-sight teaching on by default.

## Foundation

The three menu designs of the morning agree on most of the structure: Menu, Hint and Undo on the game screen; Moves closed; Resign inside Menu; an Extra sheet. That agreement is the foundation under every direction.
