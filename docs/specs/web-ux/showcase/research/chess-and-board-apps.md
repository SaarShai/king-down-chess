# Chess apps and digital board games: what to borrow

Research track of the King Down showcase. Date: 2026-10-08.

Method: reviews, official help pages, store pages and player forums, read on the web. This track did not play the games. Each finding says what its source says, and each finding has its link. Old sources are marked "old".

## Findings

### Chess apps: what they get wrong

**F1. Ads and sales banners take the board's space.** Players of the free chess.com app say that ads and upgrade banners push the board into a corner, and that an ad shows after every game. A student op-ed calls the chess.com home page a mix of buttons, bright icons and strong colours; it calls lichess clean and calm.
Sources: [unstar.app, chess apps ranked 2026](https://unstar.app/blog/chess-com-lichess-play-magnus-chesskid-dr-wolf-chess-apps-ranked-2026), [Raven Report, "The better chess website"](https://ravenreport.org/5870/oped/the-better-chess-website).

**F2. The one tool that teaches is behind a wall.** Players name Game Review (the after-game teacher) and the daily puzzle limit as the two paywalls that hurt most.
Source: [unstar.app](https://unstar.app/blog/chess-com-lichess-play-magnus-chesskid-dr-wolf-chess-apps-ranked-2026).

**F3. Controls move.** Old: in the chess.com Android app, the back and forward buttons sit at the bottom of the move list, so they move when the list grows. The same report says the settings page has options in the wrong places. King Down has the same defect today: Hint and Undo drop 95 to 243 px when you select a piece (`review.md`, item 1).
Source: [chess.com forum, UI feedback](https://www.chess.com/clubs/forum/view/ui-feedback?lc=1).

**F4. The notation is for experts.** Writers on chess argue about whether algebraic notation (Nf3) frightens new players. Figurine notation puts a piece symbol in place of the letter, so it reads in any language. King Down adds six letters that no chess player knows: A, L, G, M, S (the Beast) and O.
Sources: [Say Chess, "A defence for algebraic notation"](https://saychess.substack.com/p/a-defence-for-algebraic-notation), [Wikipedia, algebraic notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_notation_(chess)).

**F5. Clean is not the same as friendly.** Lichess has no ads and a clean screen, but players call it plain and say it has fewer guided lessons for new players.
Source: [unstar.app](https://unstar.app/blog/chess-com-lichess-play-magnus-chesskid-dr-wolf-chess-apps-ranked-2026).

**F6. Ratings and names add stress.** Lichess Zen mode hides the opponent's name, rating and chat during a game. Players use it because a high rating on the other side makes them play worse.
Source: [lichess forum, "What are the advantages of zen mode?"](https://lichess.org/forum/general-chess-discussion/what-are-the-advantages-of-zen-mode).

### Chess teachers and chess-like games: what works

**F7. Icons that you scan, and marks on the board.** Chess.com Game Review gives each move an icon: !! (brilliant), ! (great), ?? (blunder), X (miss: a chance that you did not take). The coach draws arrows and marks squares on the board, and a report card grades the opening, the middle game and the endgame.
Sources: [chess.com, Game Review v2](https://www.chess.com/news/view/chesscom-launches-game-review-v2), [chess.com help, how moves are classified](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8572705-how-are-moves-classified-what-is-a-blunder-or-brilliant-move).

**F8. A coach that speaks plain words and gives a second try.** Dr. Wolf (chess.com) explains in plain English why a move is good or bad. After a blunder it says what went wrong and asks if you want to try again; a good second try gets praise. It can suggest that you flip the board to see the mistake from the other side.
Sources: [Popular Science on Dr. Wolf](https://www.popsci.com/?p=568779), [chess.com blog, "Meet Dr. Wolf"](https://www.chess.com/blog/Sawbonez/meet-dr-wolf-1).

**F9. Stars for the shortest solve.** Lichess Learn puts its lessons in order from easy to hard and uses game rewards in place of a list of rules. Players report that fewer moves give more stars (three stars in eight moves on one step).
Sources: [lichess blog, "Something for the beginners"](https://lichess.org/blog/V5EX3CgAAIoE7aBR/something-for-the-beginners), [lichess forum, three stars](https://lichess.org/forum/general-chess-discussion/how-to-get-3-stars-in-combat-tutorial).

**F10. You play the move; you do not only read it.** Chessable MoveTrainer gives a short note on one move, then you play that move on the board. At the end of a line you play the whole line again. The site brings the line back at growing intervals, and gives points and badges. Because the answer is a move on the board, the site checks every answer.
Sources: [FIDE, "From flashcards to Chessable"](https://www.fide.com/from-flashcards-to-chessable/), [notes on MoveTrainer](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zDr94hP6bG3jJYrdYy8B5hx).

**F11. Random armies are fun when the draw has light rules.** Really Bad Chess (Zach Gage) gives each side a random army. The draw follows your rank: a new player gets strong pieces against a weak computer army, and the edge gets smaller as the rank goes up. Gage found that pure chance felt like a game with no rules, so he added light rules to the draw. The daily and weekly boards are the same for every player, with a leaderboard for the fewest moves. The free game has a limited number of undo tokens, and more tokens cost money.
Sources: [TouchArcade review](https://toucharcade.com/?p=204079), [Global Toy News, "Games that aren't fair"](https://globaltoynews.com/2022/05/31/really-bad-chess-and-games-that-arent-fair-2/), [Noodlecake, game page](https://noodlecake.com/games/really-bad-chess/).

**F12. Show the threat at the moment of the mistake.** In Shotgun King, the enemy piece that moves next shakes. Folly Shield refuses a move that walks into a capture, and the piece that threatens you flashes red. Between floors you choose a pair of cards: one helps you and one helps the enemy. One reviewer turned off the strong flash at the enemy king's defeat; another asks for clearer card text.
Sources: [Turn Based Lovers review](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/shotgun-king-the-final-checkmate-review/), [Nintendo World Report review](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/64499/shotgun-king-switch-review).

**F13. Hold an enemy to see its reach.** In Pawnbarian, cards show chess-piece silhouettes. A long tap on an enemy shows the squares that it attacks, and red notches on a square count the hits you take there. Two problems: the status words (Purify, Ward, Blight) have no explanation, so players lose runs to learn them; and a long tap while a card is selected can play the move by accident.
Source: [Pocket Gamer review](https://www.pocketgamer.com/pawnbarian/mobile-review/).

**F14. Perfect information makes turns fast.** Into the Breach (not on this track's list; the brief names it) shows every enemy attack before you act. The developers found that this made battles faster, because the player plans at once. You can undo moves before you commit, and the game also gives a limited reset of a whole turn.
Sources: [Kotaku review](https://kotaku.com/into-the-breach-the-kotaku-review-1823365331), [Wikipedia, Into the Breach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Breach).

**F15. A move as a picture.** In Onitama, each move card has a clear diagram of where a piece can go. The used card passes to the opponent, so both players see all five cards.
Sources: [Casual Game Revolution review](https://casualgamerevolution.com/node/1429), [Wikipedia, Onitama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onitama).

**F16. Say why a move is not legal.** The Hive app explains why an illegal move is illegal. Undo and hints let new players learn "without pressure", and the store page puts a clean screen first.
Source: [App Store, Hive](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6747834729).

**F17. Figures as toys, rules on the base.** Hitman GO puts figures in fixed poses, like chess pieces, on small diorama boards. A pointer on each enemy's base shows where it faces, and each enemy kind has its own colour. Bonus goals give stars, and stars open the next chapters. The team made the game by keeping only the core of the series.
Sources: [Wikipedia, Hitman Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitman_Go), [GDC, "Design by constraints"](https://gdconf.com/news/design_by_constraints).

### Digital board games: what they do well, and where they fail

**F18. Rule reminders on tap.** Wingspan has a "?" button that turns help labels on and off. A tap on a round goal shows its text. The game asks before it skips a card power, and it reminds you when you cannot pay. Root shows an option only when you can use it.
Sources: [Meeple Mountain, Wingspan digital](https://www.meeplemountain.com/reviews/wingspan-digital/), [Board Game Quest, Root](https://www.boardgamequest.com/root-digital-board-game-review/), [Meadow Party, Root app](https://meadowparty.com/blog/2020/10/30/root-app/).

**F19. Teach in play, and teach the hard rule.** Root's tutorial starts you inside a short game for each faction, then lets you finish it alone. Board Game Arena tutorials make you play set moves, so the rules come in a set order; players say the text bubbles cover the board. The Carcassonne tutorial never mentions farmers, which a reviewer calls the most important rule. Ticket to Ride does not force a tutorial at the first start, and its guide plays a whole game with you.
Sources: [Meadow Party, Root](https://meadowparty.com/blog/2020/10/30/root-app/), [BGA forum, tutorials](https://forum.boardgamearena.com/viewtopic.php?p=51548), [Meeple Mountain, Carcassonne](https://www.meeplemountain.com/reviews/carcassonne-android-app-review-the-digital-french-countryside/), [Board Game Quest, Ticket to Ride](https://boardgamequest.com/ticket-to-ride-ipad-and-iphone-review).

**F20. Undo must be there, and must be clear.** One reviewer took half a star from Root because it had no undo, and called undo the easy fix. Another reviewer found that some actions undo and others do not, with no sign of which is which.
Sources: [Board Game Quest, Root](https://www.boardgamequest.com/root-digital-board-game-review/), [Meadow Party, Root](https://meadowparty.com/blog/2020/10/30/root-app/).

**F21. The log as a row of pictures.** Hearthstone's history is a column of small pictures beside the board: the last 7 to 10 actions, newest at the top, with details on hover. A blue or red border shows whose action it is (colour only, which fails colour-blind players). On Board Game Arena you tap a line of the game log to replay the game from that point.
Sources: [Hearthstone wiki, History](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/History), [BGA, turn-based FAQ](https://en.doc.boardgamearena.com/Turn_based_FAQ).

**F22. Animation helps once, then it costs time.** Wingspan slides each player's avatar as the round bonuses add up. Ticket to Ride plays a short animation when you claim a route. But reviewers tell Root players to turn on quick battles, because the battle animation gets old; Carcassonne gives no way to turn its animations off during a game; and Shotgun King's flash was too bright for one reviewer.
Sources: [Meeple Mountain, Wingspan](https://www.meeplemountain.com/reviews/wingspan-digital/), [Board Game Quest, Ticket to Ride](https://boardgamequest.com/ticket-to-ride-ipad-and-iphone-review), [Board Game Quest, Root](https://www.boardgamequest.com/root-digital-board-game-review/), [Meeple Mountain, Carcassonne](https://www.meeplemountain.com/reviews/carcassonne-android-app-review-the-digital-french-countryside/).

**F23. The ending tells the story and proves it was fair.** Wingspan adds the final score one part at a time for each player. Catan Universe shows the spread of the dice rolls at the end, so players can see that the dice were fair. Chess.com gives a report card (F7).
Sources: [Meeple Mountain, Wingspan](https://www.meeplemountain.com/reviews/wingspan-digital/), [The Board Game Family, Catan Universe](https://www.theboardgamefamily.com/2018/06/catans-digital-rebirth-catan-universe/).

**F24. Play at your own pace.** On Board Game Arena, turn-based players do not need to be online at the same time. An email or your next visit tells you it is your turn. The pace goes from 10 moves a day to 1, and you are asked to play several games at once. To catch up, you replay from a log line (F21). In Ticket to Ride online, a computer player takes the seat of a player who leaves.
Sources: [BGA, turn-based FAQ](https://en.doc.boardgamearena.com/Turn_based_FAQ), [Board Game Quest, Ticket to Ride](https://boardgamequest.com/ticket-to-ride-ipad-and-iphone-review).

**F25. Small switches, not a wall of settings.** Carcassonne keeps extra views as switches: a list of the tiles still to come and a button that shows who holds each field. Its "Meeple Finder" makes placed figures hop now and then so you can find them. But folding can go too far: in Wingspan you must hunt for the other players' boards.
Sources: [Meeple Mountain, Carcassonne](https://www.meeplemountain.com/reviews/carcassonne-android-app-review-the-digital-french-countryside/), [Meeple Mountain, Wingspan](https://www.meeplemountain.com/reviews/wingspan-digital/).

**F26. A good game in a clumsy frame.** Reviewers of Catan Universe praise the 3D table and the opponents' status at the top, but trade requests pile up and break the pace, and touch control fails on small phones.
Source: [The Board Game Family, Catan Universe](https://www.theboardgamefamily.com/2018/06/catans-digital-rebirth-catan-universe/).

### Patterns across the sources

1. **The board is the interface.** The best games put help on the board (marks, arrows, rings) and not in a side panel: F7, F12, F13, F14, F17.
2. **Show cause, not only result.** A refused move or a lost piece points to the piece that did it: F12, F14, F16.
3. **Teach the piece when you meet it**, inside a real game, and teach the hard rule: F9, F10, F13, F19.
4. **Pictures beat notation** for a log that a new player reads: F4, F7, F15, F21.
5. **Animation is a cost.** One short effect per event, and a way to skip it: F12, F22.
6. **Fold, but do not hide what a player needs to see every turn**: F6, F25.
7. **Undo is trust.** Free, clear and never sold: F11 (the warning), F14, F16, F20.

## For King Down

Fifteen design moves. Each says what it borrows, what the player sees, when, and for how long. Each has a default: **on** (always there), **quiet** (there, small) or **folded** (in Extras). All motion follows the brief: it shows a change, a cause or a place; it respects `prefers-reduced-motion`; and the player's next tap or key ends it at its last frame. No move changes a rule; where a move shows a rule, the words come from `docs/RULES.md` and the captions in `src/moment.ts`.

### K1. The draw: one strong moment at the start (on)

- **Borrows:** Really Bad Chess (F11: the random army is the fun), Catan Universe (F23: show that chance was fair).
- **Sees:** the board opens with the kings and pawns in place. The seven drawn figures land on the back rank, from file a to file h. Each Black figure lands at the same time as its White twin, so the player sees, with no words, that both sides get the same army. Then one quiet line: "Same army for both sides."
- **When:** a new game with a random army, and Rematch with a new army. Not on Rematch with the same army, and not when a saved game opens.
- **How long:** 60 ms between figures, 240 ms for each landing: about 700 ms. A tap or a key ends it at once, and the board takes input at once. Reduced motion: one 150 ms fade.
- **Future:** card mode uses the same beat to show the cards dealt; an unlocked piece (PROGRESSION.md) lands last, with K7's spark.

### K2. Hold any piece to see its reach (on)

- **Borrows:** Pawnbarian (F13: long tap on an enemy), Into the Breach (F14: see the threat before you act).
- **Sees:** hold a figure (yours or the enemy's) for 300 ms on touch, or point at it for 400 ms with a mouse. Its moves show as dots, its captures as rings, and an archer's shots as crosshairs: three shapes, so colour is not the only sign. Enemy marks use the enemy's ink. The piece card (with its glyph, K3) shows in the context area. Release, and the marks fade in 120 ms. With the keyboard, the cursor on a square shows the same after 400 ms.
- **When:** at any time, also during the computer's turn and in review.
- **Guard rail:** a hold never moves a piece. Only a separate tap moves (the Pawnbarian accident in F13). This also fixes defect D-10 (a tap on an enemy gives a refusal today).

### K3. One move glyph for each piece (on)

- **Borrows:** Onitama (F15: the move as a diagram on the card), chess.com icons (F7).
- **Sees:** a 5×5 grid icon for each piece. Dots are steps, rings are captures, a crosshair is a shot, a two-way arrow is the Maester swap, a push arrow is the Ogre shove, a shield is the Guard. The Archer's glyph shows exactly its RULES §3 squares: the diagonal neighbours, two squares straight, and two squares on the forward diagonals.
- **Where:** 64 px on the piece card, the Guide and the lessons; 20 px in the move story (K6).
- **Future:** a Workshop piece gets its glyph from its own rules, so a shared piece has a face in the link preview. Proposed pieces (PIECES-PROPOSED.md) and power cards use the same marks.

### K4. "Why not?" points at the cause (on)

- **Borrows:** Shotgun King's Folly Shield (F12: the threatening piece flashes), Hive (F16: say why a move is illegal).
- **Sees:** you tap a square, but the move would leave your king in check. Nothing moves. The attacking piece gets two short ink pulses (2 × 160 ms), and a thin line draws from it to your king in 220 ms. The line stays until your next tap. The context area says it in words, for example: "Your king would be in check from the archer on c6. An archer shoots over pieces." The line and the words carry the meaning, not the colour.
- **Other refusals, same shape:** a Guard ("Only a king can take a guard."), a frozen piece ("This piece is frozen. It cannot move this turn."), a sheltered piece (the Holy Light or Mercy squares light up around their king).
- **When:** every refused move. No sound, no dialog.

### K5. Effects live on the figure's base (on)

- **Borrows:** Hitman GO (F17: the rule is on the base), Into the Breach (F14: effects stay in view).
- **Sees:** a frozen piece has a ring of ice on its base and a small "1" for the turn left. A piece under Ice Wall has a ward ring. An always-on aura (Holy Light, Mercy) shows its squares only while you hold the king (K2), never all the time. A spent one-use power (Strike, Flight) shows as an empty pip on the king's strip.
- **How long:** the ring grows in 200 ms when the effect starts and thaws in 300 ms when it ends. This agrees with motion item F3 of `review.md`.
- **Future:** card effects (Firewall, Rescue) use the same rings, so a new card needs no new screen.

### K6. The move story: a folded strip of pictures (quiet; full list folded)

- **Borrows:** Hearthstone's history (F21: small pictures, newest first), Board Game Arena (F21: tap a log line to replay), chess.com icons (F7).
- **Sees:** one row under the strips with the last five moves as chips: the figure, a verb mark, the square (for example: archer, crosshair, c5). The verb marks come from the move kinds in `src/moment.ts`: move, take, shot, chain, swap, shove, freeze. Your chips are filled and the opponent's chips have an outline, so the side does not depend on colour. A new chip slides in from the right in 180 ms. A tap on a chip opens review at that move.
- **Open the story** (one tap): the whole game as short sentences with the same marks, for example "Your beast took the knight on d5, then the pawn on e6." Key moments carry a star. Letter notation (Nf3) is a switch in Extras, off by default.
- **Why:** this is the owner's request: the transcript folds, and it reads like a story with icons.

### K7. First meeting: a new piece teaches itself in a real game (on, once per piece)

- **Borrows:** Root (F19: start inside a real game), Ticket to Ride (F19: no forced tutorial), and two warnings: Carcassonne skips the hard rule, Pawnbarian never explains its words (F13, F19).
- **Sees:** a random army has a King Down piece that you never played. In the draw (K1) that figure lands last and keeps a small spark on its base. One line under the board: "New: the Beast. After it takes, it may take again." with its glyph. Tap the spark to see its reach (K2).
- **How long:** the line stays until your first move. The spark stays until you move that piece, or for three games. No timer and no dialog.
- **Future:** the unlock moment of PROGRESSION.md becomes this meeting. In a link game, a piece that only your friend unlocked meets you the same way.

### K8. Lesson stars for the shortest solve (quiet)

- **Borrows:** lichess Learn (F9), Hitman GO stars (F17), Chessable (F10: you play the move).
- **Sees:** each lesson task ends with one to three stars: one for done, three for the fewest moves. "Try for three stars" is a quiet link; Play stays the main button (decision W4). No fail screen and no timer.
- **Rule of words:** stars buy nothing. Crowns (PROGRESSION.md) stay the only unlock count, so each word keeps one meaning.
- **Optional, folded:** a few days later the title can show one small card: "Can you still find the Maester swap?" It is never a notification and never counts days.

### K9. A second look at Beginner and Casual (on at Beginner; switch in Extras)

- **Borrows:** Dr. Wolf (F8: say what went wrong, offer a second try, praise the fix), Into the Breach undo (F14).
- **Sees:** your move leaves your queen free to take, or lets the computer mate next move. The computer waits. The board draws the line from the attacker (as in K4), and the context area says: "Your queen on d4 is open to the archer on f6." with two buttons: "Take it back" and "Keep it". A good second move gets one quiet word: "Better."
- **When:** only for these two large mistakes, so a fast player sees it seldom. It has no timer.
- **Why:** a first win is the best first memory (decision W1). Undo is free and never sold (the warning in F11).

### K10. The "?" lens: names on everything, only while you want them (quiet)

- **Borrows:** Wingspan (F18: one "?" button turns help labels on and off).
- **Sees:** one 44 px "?" button in the toolbar. Press it: each figure gets its name on a small parchment tag under it, each king strip shows its power in words, and the board edge shows the file and rank letters. Press again, or Escape, to close. On a phone you can also hold it to see and release to hide. The tags fade in 150 ms.
- **Why:** six new pieces need names in place, not in a guide. This replaces the piece-letter setting for most players (defect D-8).

### K11. A board-first ending with three key moments (on)

- **Borrows:** Wingspan (F22, F23: the score builds one part at a time), chess.com (F7: icons and arrows on key moves), Board Game Arena (F21: replay from a log line).
- **Sees:** the king topples (it exists today). 600 ms later a small card rises over the lower third of the board, never over the fallen king: "You win! Checkmate on move 23." Then up to three key-moment chips come in, 150 ms apart, each with a mark and a few words: "Move 14: your beast took two pieces." A tap on a chip shows that position and plays the move once (400 ms) with an arrow. Buttons: Rematch (main), Review, New game (decision W8). One fact line can follow: "Your first win with an ogre."
- **Folded:** no accuracy score and no grade on this card. Those judge a new player; Review can hold them later.

### K12. The daily board: one army for everyone (quiet)

- **Borrows:** Really Bad Chess (F11: the same daily board for every player, the fewest moves).
- **Sees:** the title shows today's army as a row of eight small figures, a tease of the pieces you can meet. A win shows "Won in 31 moves." Share (it exists today) gives the date, the army, the result and the move count, with no spoilers of the moves.
- **No pressure:** no streak, no "you missed yesterday". Past dailies sit in a folded calendar in Extras.

### K13. Link games: "while you were away" (on)

- **Borrows:** Board Game Arena (F24: play at your pace, catch up from the log).
- **Sees:** you open a link game where your friend has moved. The board shows the position before their move, then plays their move once (400 ms) with the last-move wash. The story line says it in words: "Sam's ogre shoved your pawn to e5." Then "Your move." If more moves came in (online play, later), the chips play in order, 400 ms each, and a tap skips to now.
- **Future:** online play adds a list on the title, one row for each game, "Your move" rows first. No clocks by default (`review.md`, item 19).

### K14. Power cards that show their effect before you play them (later: card mode)

- **Borrows:** Into the Breach (F14: see the effect first), Onitama (F15: cards with diagrams), Pawnbarian (F13: silhouettes; a hold must not play), Shotgun King (F12: clear card text).
- **Sees:** your hand of up to six cards sits under your strip, each with a glyph (K3) and a name. Tap a card: it lifts 8 px in 180 ms and the board marks its legal targets. Tap a target to play it; tap the card again to put it back. A played card becomes a chip in the move story (K6). Each card text is one sentence, the same sentence as RULES §5.
- **Open question:** whether the opponent's hand is open is a rule question, not a design one. If it is open, show both hands, as Onitama does; if not, show only the number of cards.

### K15. A motion budget, with skip by play (rule for every screen)

- **Borrows the lessons of:** Root (F22: battle animations get old), Carcassonne (F22: no way to turn effects off in a game), Shotgun King (F12: a flash too bright).
- **Budget:** a move animates in 300 ms or less; a power, a shot or a Beast chain in 500 ms or less; one large moment per game (the king's fall) in 900 ms or less. Nothing flashes the full screen.
- **Skip:** the player's next tap or key ends any running animation at its last frame and acts at once. A "Quick" switch in Extras halves all times. Reduced motion uses short fades only.

## What to fold into Extras (from the findings)

Letter notation (F4), board coordinates outside the "?" lens (K10), the second-look switch (K9), the daily calendar (K12), the "Quick" motion switch (K15) and the threat markers of today's Settings. Nothing that a player needs every turn goes there (F25).
