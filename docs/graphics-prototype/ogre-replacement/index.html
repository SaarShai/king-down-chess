What the mesh must preserve

  • A small head sitting low between wide, sloping shoulders.
  • A connected shoulder–chest–belly mass, with no ball-shaped muscle joints.
  • A heavy lower jaw, recessed eyes and a broad blunt nose.
  • Short sturdy legs, wide feet and large, flattened hands.
  • Terracotta pads sculpted as separate clay on the pushing surfaces.

The sheet varies slightly between views, especially around the hand pads. Establish one consistent neutral pose before reconstruction.

Recommended for fidelity

Control the views, then sculpt the result.

An image-to-3D pass gives us a base shaped by the actual character. Blender cleanup can then concentrate on the face, continuous shoulder mass, hands and animation joints.

  1. Keep the prepared transparent single-view reference as the character authority. Establish consistent front, side and back views, including the head height and exact hand-pad placement.
  2. Try those views through Tripo's multiview workflow, or adapt Klayzen's official mesh. Inspect the geometry with a neutral material first.
  3. Correct the sculpt in Blender. Build the coloured hand pads as exact separate surfaces.
  4. Compare front, side and game-camera views beside Guard and Beast at board size. Rig only after the body and face read correctly.
Practical access and animation limits

Tripo needs a service account and credits; its free tier is public/noncommercial. TRELLIS.2's public demo generated our approved test preview but failed GLB export. Its local release requires a Linux/NVIDIA GPU setup. No purchase or additional service upload has been made.

Generated geometry is a sculpting base. Knees, elbows, fingers and clothing still need inspection, suitable topology, weights and walk/shove clips. Our existing Three.js, clay materials, capture effects and board-mesh pipeline can accept the resulting GLB.
