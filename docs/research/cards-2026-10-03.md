# Card mode — first measurements and a proposed card set (2026-10-03)

Owner (2026-10-03): "we should just have action/effect cards, not pieces cards … i'm thinking each
player gets the same collection of random cards (6 for example) which they can use throughout the
game, once per card. but there might be better options." Then: "do the measurements you suggest.
also, cards CAN change the way pieces move/take … it is a possibility to consider when considering
rules updates for existing cards and for new cards."

## Summary

- **Every card is worth something.** Played by one side against no card, a card is worth from
  half a pawn (March) to more than two (Haste), and no card raised draws in those games
  (measurement 1).
- **With the same hand for both sides, cards remove most of White's first-move edge.** White scores
  55.4% without cards and 50.5% ± 1.9 with them (3 to 6 cards together); the hand sizes do not
  clearly differ.
- **Cards about halve the draws** (16.5% → 7–10%) **and shorten the games** (102 turns → 77.5
  with six cards; among hands of 3 to 6 cards, about 3 turns less for each extra card).
- **Eight cards beat six on draws and length** (4.1% draws against 6.8%, about 7 turns shorter),
  and are as fair; seven play like six. **The four new cards fit the deal**: six cards from all
  twelve play like six from the eight (measurement 4).
- **Recommendation: eight cards dealt from all twelve**; six if eight are too many to keep in mind.
- Eleven new cards are proposed, most of which change how pieces move or take, rated by the work to
  build them; four are suggested first (Mimic, Vault, Curse, Sky Lift).

## How cards are played (the proposal measured here)

- Both players get the **same hand**, face up: k cards dealt at random from a pool. Fair in the same
  way as the shared random army, and open, so the computer player plans with the cards.
- Each card is played **once**. A side plays **at most one card a turn**: the card is the turn's move,
  or (Freeze, Ice Wall) a free action and then the ordinary move (or end the turn).
- No card captures or freezes a king.
- In the measurements the first four plies are random piece moves (as in every tournament), so no
  card is played in the opening; the rule "no card on the first move" makes no difference there.

## The cards measured: one use of each spendable king power

These already exist in the engine, with the official readings of the kings' powers mode. March is
the exception: its official reading is always on, so the March card uses the printed counted
reading (one double step from any rank, one use).

| card | effect | original card (2014 cards; undated rulebook drafts) |
|---|---|---|
| Freeze | an enemy piece (not the king) cannot move on the opponent's next turn; then your move | Frost Bite (enemies in range cannot move) |
| Ice Wall | an own piece (not the king) cannot be taken on the opponent's next turn; then your move | Shield (taking costs more) |
| Strike | a piece (not a pawn) moves as a queen to an empty square, as the turn | Strike (take without moving) |
| Haste | one piece moves twice in the turn (the second move may be skipped); neither move takes | Haste (2014 card: +1 action; rulebook draft: copy another piece's range) |
| Flight | a piece (not the king) moves to any empty square of its own half | Flight (move a piece anywhere) |
| Sacrifice | a pawn becomes one of your captured pieces (not a guard), on its square | Sacrifice (swap with a captured friendly piece) / Salvation |
| March | a pawn makes a double step from any rank | — (2017 king power) |
| Leap | a rook, bishop or queen passes over its own pawns | Leap (range not obstructed) |

## Measurement 1: each card against no card

One side holds a single card, the other none; 300 colour-swapped pairs per card, each on its own
army (5,400 games with the no-card baseline; `sim/out/cards-a1.report.md`). A card here is the king
power with one use, which is the same move set as a one-card hand.

| card | score vs no card | value | draws | Δ draws vs no cards |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Haste | 70.0% ± 3.5 | +2.3 ± 0.5 pawns | 9.7% | −10.0 ± 5.1 |
| Strike | 66.8% ± 3.4 | +1.9 ± 0.4 | 14.0% | −5.7 ± 5.3 |
| Freeze | 64.8% ± 3.4 | +1.7 ± 0.4 | 13.0% | −6.7 ± 5.3 |
| Flight | 63.4% ± 3.5 | +1.5 ± 0.4 | 14.8% | −4.8 ± 5.4 |
| Sacrifice | 61.2% ± 3.8 | +1.2 ± 0.4 | 14.3% | −5.3 ± 5.3 |
| Leap | 59.9% ± 3.3 | +1.1 ± 0.4 | 15.5% | −4.2 ± 5.4 |
| Ice Wall | 55.5% ± 3.6 | +0.6 ± 0.4 | 16.0% | −3.7 ± 5.5 |
| March | 55.0% ± 3.5 | +0.5 ± 0.4 | 14.3% | −5.3 ± 5.3 |

Games without cards end drawn 19.7% ± 4.5 of the time. This baseline is 300 games on their own
armies; measurement 2's 800 no-card games draw 16.5%, and against that figure the drops are about 3
points smaller (Haste −6.8, Ice Wall −0.5). **Every card is worth something** (every score interval
excludes 50%), and no card raised draws here, but three cards' intervals allow a small rise, and part
of each drop comes from the imbalance itself (one side is stronger). Measurement 2 gives the clean
draw reading. Haste is worth several times March (about four times at the point estimates; the
ratio is loose because March's value is close to zero).
In a same-hand game the spread in value is not a fairness problem (both sides hold the same cards),
but a card worth half a pawn may feel like a dud next to one worth two.

## Measurement 2: the same hand for both sides

Both sides hold the same k cards, dealt at random for each pair from the eight. A smaller hand is
the first cards of a larger one, and every hand size plays the same 800 armies, one game each
(`cards-b2`: none, 3 and 6 cards; `cards-b3`: 4 and 5; 4,000 games;
`sim/out/cards-b2+cards-b3.report.md`). Intervals are 95% over armies; Δ is the difference from no
cards on the same armies.

| cards | White's score | draws | turns | Δ White | Δ draws | Δ turns | cards played per side |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 55.4% ± 3.2 | 16.5% ± 2.6 | 101.7 ± 3.3 | — | — | — | 0 |
| 3 | 49.8% ± 3.3 | 8.6% ± 1.9 | 86.4 ± 3.2 | −5.6 ± 4.6 | −7.9 ± 3.1 | −15.3 ± 4.6 | 2.41 |
| 4 | 54.1% ± 3.3 | 9.9% ± 2.1 | 83.0 ± 3.5 | −1.3 ± 4.4 | −6.6 ± 3.3 | −18.6 ± 4.7 | 3.08 |
| 5 | 48.3% ± 3.3 | 7.6% ± 1.8 | 80.7 ± 3.2 | −7.1 ± 4.5 | −8.9 ± 3.1 | −21.0 ± 4.6 | 3.79 |
| 6 | 50.0% ± 3.4 | 6.8% ± 1.7 | 77.5 ± 3.0 | −5.4 ± 4.5 | −9.8 ± 3.1 | −24.2 ± 4.4 | 4.43 |

- **The first-move edge goes.** Over the four hand sizes together, White scores 50.5% ± 1.9, a
  drop of 4.8 ± 3.5 points from no cards on the same armies. The hand sizes do not clearly differ:
  a permutation test across the four gives p = 0.07, and White's score has no trend with the
  number of cards (−0.5 ± 1.5 points a card). Four cards read highest (54.1%, 4.7 ± 3.6 above the
  mean of the other three), but that comparison was picked after seeing the data; a rerun on new
  armies would show whether it is real.
- **Draws about halve with any hand** (40–60% fewer), and may fall a little as the hand grows
  (−0.8 ± 0.8 points a card, not clear; four cards read higher than three; six cards against three:
  −1.9 ± 2.6). This is the clean draw reading that measurement 1 could not give: here both sides
  are equally strong.
- **Games get shorter with every card**: −2.9 ± 1.4 turns a card; six cards against three: −8.9 ±
  4.3 turns.
- **Most cards get played** (share of held cards played, over the four hand sizes): Freeze,
  Flight and Sacrifice 91–96%, Ice Wall 84–91%, Haste 61–77%, Strike 60–69%, Leap 61–62%, March
  45–57%. Leap is played early (median ply 17), Haste and March late (median ply 46–56). The share
  falls a little as the hand grows: 80% of three cards, 74% of six.

## Measurement 3: the first four new cards

Owner (2026-10-03, on the proposed cards): "do whichever you suggest". Mimic, Vault, Curse and Sky
Lift are built as card-only cards (rules in the table below); each held alone against no card, 300
colour-swapped pairs, every card on the same 300 armies as the no-card mirror games (`cards-a2`,
seed 5454, 3,000 games; `sim/out/cards-a2.report.md`).

| card | score vs no card | value | played in | draws | Δ draws vs no cards |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vault | 63.4% ± 3.6 | +1.5 ± 0.4 pawns | 72% of games | 14.5% | −4.2 ± 5.3 |
| Sky Lift | 60.6% ± 3.6 | +1.2 ± 0.4 | 74% | 18.2% | −0.5 ± 5.4 |
| Mimic | 59.6% ± 3.7 | +1.1 ± 0.4 | 79% | 13.2% | −5.5 ± 5.1 |
| Curse | 52.4% ± 3.6 | +0.3 ± 0.4 | 90% | 16.2% | −2.5 ± 5.3 |

Games without cards drew 18.7% ± 4.4 here. Vault, Sky Lift and Mimic are worth about a pawn or
more, in the range of Flight, Sacrifice and Leap in measurement 1 (other armies, so compare loosely).
**Curse is not clearly worth anything** (its interval reaches 50%), although the computer plays it
in 90% of games: at depth 3 it spends the card on small gains. No new card raised draws in these
games; as in measurement 1, part of each drop is the imbalance.

## Measurement 4: seven and eight cards, and the four new cards in the deal

Owner: "is 6 the optimal one for white-black balance and draws and quicker games?" Seven and eight
cards (`cards-b4`, 1,600 games: 8 parts on this Mac, 5 on Kaggle, 3 more here), and six cards dealt
from all twelve (the eight powers plus Mimic, Vault, Curse and Sky Lift; `cards-b5`, 800 games), on
the same 800 armies as measurement 2 (`sim/out/cards-b2+cards-b3+cards-b4.report.md`,
`sim/out/cards-b5.report.md`).

| cards | White's score | draws | turns | cards played per side |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 55.4% ± 3.2 | 16.5% ± 2.6 | 101.7 ± 3.3 | 0 |
| 6 | 50.0% ± 3.4 | 6.8% ± 1.7 | 77.5 ± 3.0 | 4.43 |
| 7 | 48.6% ± 3.4 | 6.5% ± 1.7 | 75.2 ± 3.0 | 5.03 |
| 8 | 52.1% ± 3.4 | 4.1% ± 1.4 | 70.6 ± 2.7 | 5.62 |
| 6 from all twelve | 50.4% ± 3.4 | 6.9% ± 1.8 | 77.0 ± 3.2 | 4.32 |

Against six cards (from the eight) on the same armies:

| | White's score | draws | turns |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 cards | −1.4 ± 4.3 | −0.2 ± 2.4 | −2.3 ± 3.9 |
| 8 cards | +2.1 ± 4.5 | −2.6 ± 2.2 | −6.8 ± 3.8 |
| 6 from all twelve | +0.4 ± 4.6 | +0.1 ± 2.5 | −0.4 ± 4.1 |

- **Eight cards beat six on draws and length**: 2.6 ± 2.2 points fewer draws (4.1% against 6.8%)
  and about 7 turns shorter. White's score stays level with Black's within the intervals at every
  size from 5 to 8. Seven cards play like six. Eight is the most a hand
  holds (the engine's limit).
- **The four new cards fit the deal**: six cards from all twelve play like six from the eight on
  White's score, draws and length.

## Recommendation

- **Hand size: eight** for the fewest draws and the shortest games (measurement 4), as fair as six;
  **six** if eight cards are too many for a player to keep in mind (the owner's first idea; as fair,
  2.6 points more draws, 7 turns longer). Deal from all twelve cards (measurement 4). These numbers describe
  the computer player at depth 3; people may play cards differently.
- **The pool:** March and Ice Wall are the weakest cards (+0.5 and +0.6 pawns; the measurement
  cannot separate them). March is also played least (about half the time), so it is the first
  candidate to replace with a new card.
- **Next:** build the new cards the owner picks, measure each against no card as in measurement
  1, then a same-hand round with the larger pool. Card mode is not in the playable game yet.

## New cards (most change how pieces move; owner: allowed for cards)

Original cards, most of which change how pieces move or take, a chess reading for each, and the work to build it, for the owner to
pick from. None is built yet; each needs its own measurement. The build ratings come from a
read-only review of the engine with an adversarial check (2026-10-03). Small: under about 100
lines and tests, no new game state. Medium: new state, or a new kind of move.

| card | proposed reading | build | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mimic** (Haste: copy the range of a unit in play) | one of your pieces (not the king, not a pawn) moves this turn as another piece type on the board | small (upper edge) | The temp 2.0 draft copies a friendly unit's range, for moves only (temp 4.0: any unit in play); the narrower reading is the simpler one. With captures and a queen on the board it contains Strike with captures (59% as a king power), so start without captures. |
| **Vault** (Leap: range not obstructed) | a rook, bishop or queen passes over one piece, own or enemy | small | The source allows any number of pieces in the way. Stronger than today's Leap card (over own pawns only). |
| **Control** (control a friendly unit within range; it gets the controller's range) | a piece moves this turn with the range of a friendly piece next to it | small | The next-to reading narrows the source; the source reading is a friendly piece whose own moves reach the mover. Next-to is often useless: it needs a friendly piece of another type next to the mover. It could be a narrower Mimic instead of a card of its own. |
| **Rage** (+1 move / +1 take) | one piece moves twice and may take on either move | medium | This is the rulebook's Haste, which won 80% as a king power: likely too strong. Softer: the second move must be a capture. |
| **Burn** (take an enemy unit in the capital) | take an enemy piece (not the king) on d4, e4, d5 or e5 with a piece that reaches it as a queen | small | Useless while no enemy piece stands in the centre. |
| **Fire Starter** (take enemy units in the first row) | a piece may take on the enemy back rank as if it were a queen | small | One generator serves Burn and Fire Starter (a zone each). Large late swings. |
| **Curse** (control an enemy unit of the same type) | move one enemy pawn or piece (not the king) one step | small | The step may not expose your own king; the fast legality test must check it. |
| **Earth Quake** (move units in range) | the pieces next to a square are pushed one step outwards, where empty | medium | Several pieces move in one turn (three code paths must agree). About 16 such moves exist even at the start. |
| **Sky Lift** (swap with a unit in range) | swap two of your own pieces (not the king, not pawns) | small | It may be stronger than Flight, which lands only in your own half. |
| **Salvation** (revive a taken piece) | return one of your captured pieces to an empty square of your back rank | medium | The first move that starts off the board. Likely among the strongest: a free piece, about Sacrifice (+1.2 pawns as one card, measurement 1) plus the pawn kept, so near Haste (+2.3); Rage is likely stronger still. |
| **Mirror** (copy a card) | play the card your opponent played last | medium | With the same hand for both sides it is mostly a duplicate. The Black Mirror variant's "copy any card in your hand" needs no new state; nor does the 2014 text "copy any played card". |

Suggested first: **Mimic, Vault, Curse and Sky Lift**. Each is small to build (Mimic at the upper
edge). Curse and Sky Lift play unlike the eight cards measured here; Vault and Mimic widen Leap and
Strike. Building any of them needs one shared change (card-only names beside the
king powers) and a legality cross-check with random hands, the one test that catches a card which
lets a side leave its own king in check.

## Method

- Depth 3 search, the linear evaluation, four random opening plies, a fresh army for every pair
  (`--armies perPair`). Intervals are 95%, over armies. Rules: the official readings of the
  spendable powers (Freeze and Ice Wall a free action, Haste without captures, Strike pieces only
  without captures), each card one use.
- A game ends when one side leads by 6 pawns for 3 plies in a row (resign; 89.5% of the 4,000
  same-hand games, 91.0% of those with cards), or by the usual draws, including a quiet-position draw after ply 68.
- Pawns: score converted to Elo, then at the balance lab's 64 Elo per pawn (depth 3, measured
  2026-09-13 with ±25% of its own), so a pawn value carries that uncertainty too.
- Compute: the Mac and up to 4 Kaggle notebooks at once (the account allows 5), sharing one
  commit per run; Kaggle games are identical to the Mac's move for move.
- Tools: `src/sim/tournament.ts` (`--anchor`, `cards<k>`, `--mirrorOnly`), `tools/kaggle-tournament.mjs`.
- Raw games: branch `claude/kp2-results-r13-cards`; reports and specs in `sim/out/`.
