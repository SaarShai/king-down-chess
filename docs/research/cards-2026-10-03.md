# Card mode — first measurements and a proposed card set (2026-10-03)

Owner (2026-10-03): "we should just have action/effect cards, not pieces cards … i'm thinking each
player gets the same collection of random cards (6 for example) which they can use throughout the
game, once per card. but there might be better options." Then: "do the measurements you suggest.
also, cards CAN change the way pieces move/take … it is a possibility to consider when considering
rules updates for existing cards and for new cards."

## Summary

- **Every card is worth something.** Played by one side against no card, a card is worth from
  half a pawn (March) to more than two (Haste), and no card raised draws in those games
  (measurement 1).
- **With the same hand for both sides, cards remove most of White's first-move edge.** White scores
  55.4% without cards and 50.5% ± 1.9 with them (3 to 6 cards together); the hand sizes do not
  clearly differ.
- **Cards about halve the draws** (16.5% → 7–10%) **and shorten the games** (102 turns → 77.5
  with six cards; among hands of 3 to 6 cards, about 3 turns less for each extra card).
- **Eight cards beat six on draws and length** (4.1% draws against 6.8%, about 7 turns shorter),
  and are as fair; seven play like six. **The four new cards fit the deal**: six cards from all
  twelve play like six from the eight (measurement 4).
- **Owner decision (2026-10-03): six cards**, "the most important is to eliminate white's advantage and to not have too many cards". Suggested: deal them from all twelve.
- Eleven new cards are proposed, most of which change how pieces move or take, rated by the work to
  build them; four are suggested first (Mimic, Vault, Curse, Sky Lift).
- **All of them are built now** (2026-10-04, lab): Salvation, and the 2014 cards Rage, Mirror,
  Firewall, Earth Quake, Burn, Fire Starter, Control, Rescue and Growth, five with a softer `B`
  reading. Rescue as worded can never be played (its mark has ended by its side's next turn), so it
  renews the mark instead. Measurement 5 (run 2026-10-04) measured each against no card: Rage is
  the one overpowered card (+4.06 pawns, about 1.7 Hastes) and RageB is not softer; MirrorB works
  and Mirror does not; Rescue adds nothing measurable.

## How cards are played (the proposal measured here)

- Both players get the **same hand**, face up: k cards dealt at random from a pool. Fair in the same
  way as the shared random army, and open, so the computer player plans with the cards.
- Each card is played **once**. A side plays **at most one card a turn**: the card is the turn's move,
  or (Freeze, Ice Wall) a free action and then the ordinary move (or end the turn).
- No card captures or freezes a king.
- In the measurements the first four plies are random piece moves (as in every tournament), so no
  card is played in the opening; the rule "no card on the first move" makes no difference there.

## The cards measured: one use of each spendable king power

These already exist in the engine, with the official readings of the kings' powers mode. March is
the exception: its official reading is always on, so the March card uses the printed counted
reading (one double step from any rank, one use).

| card | effect | original card (2014 cards; undated rulebook drafts) |
|---|---|---|
| Freeze | an enemy piece (not the king) cannot move on the opponent's next turn; then your move | Frost Bite (enemies in range cannot move) |
| Ice Wall | an own piece (not the king) cannot be taken on the opponent's next turn; then your move | Shield (taking costs more) |
| Strike | a piece (not a pawn) moves as a queen to an empty square, as the turn | Strike (take without moving) |
| Haste | one piece moves twice in the turn (the second move may be skipped); neither move takes | Haste (2014 card: +1 action; rulebook draft: copy another piece's range) |
| Flight | a piece (not the king) moves to any empty square of its own half | Flight (move a piece anywhere) |
| Sacrifice | a pawn becomes one of your captured pieces (not a guard), on its square | Sacrifice (swap with a captured friendly piece) / Salvation |
| March | a pawn makes a double step from any rank | — (2017 king power) |
| Leap | a rook, bishop or queen passes over its own pawns | Leap (range not obstructed) |

## Measurement 1: each card against no card

One side holds a single card, the other none; 300 colour-swapped pairs per card, each on its own
army (5,400 games with the no-card baseline; `sim/out/cards-a1.report.md`). A card here is the king
power with one use, which is the same move set as a one-card hand.

| Haste | 70.0% ± 3.5 | +2.3 ± 0.5 pawns | 9.7% | −10.0 ± 5.1 |
| Strike | 66.8% ± 3.4 | +1.9 ± 0.4 | 14.0% | −5.7 ± 5.3 |
| Freeze | 64.8% ± 3.4 | +1.7 ± 0.4 | 13.0% | −6.7 ± 5.3 |
| Flight | 63.4% ± 3.5 | +1.5 ± 0.4 | 14.8% | −4.8 ± 5.4 |
| Sacrifice | 61.2% ± 3.8 | +1.2 ± 0.4 | 14.3% | −5.3 ± 5.3 |
| Leap | 59.9% ± 3.3 | +1.1 ± 0.4 | 15.5% | −4.2 ± 5.4 |
| Ice Wall | 55.5% ± 3.6 | +0.6 ± 0.4 | 16.0% | −3.7 ± 5.5 |
| March | 55.0% ± 3.5 | +0.5 ± 0.4 | 14.3% | −5.3 ± 5.3 |

Games without cards end drawn 19.7% ± 4.5 of the time. This baseline is 300 games on their own
armies; measurement 2's 800 no-card games draw 16.5%, and against that figure the drops are about 3
points smaller (Haste −6.8, Ice Wall −0.5). **Every card is worth something** (every score interval
excludes 50%), and no card raised draws here, but three cards' intervals allow a small rise, and part
of each drop comes from the imbalance itself (one side is stronger). Measurement 2 gives the clean
draw reading. Haste is worth several times March (about four times at the point estimates; the
ratio is loose because March's value is close to zero).
In a same-hand game the spread in value is not a fairness problem (both sides hold the same cards),
but a card worth half a pawn may feel like a dud next to one worth two.

- **The first-move edge goes.** Over the four hand sizes together, White scores 50.5% ± 1.9, a
  drop of 4.8 ± 3.5 points from no cards on the same armies. The hand sizes do not clearly differ:
  a permutation test across the four gives p = 0.07, and White's score has no trend with the
  number of cards (−0.5 ± 1.5 points a card). Four cards read highest (54.1%, 4.7 ± 3.6 above the
  mean of the other three), but that comparison was picked after seeing the data; a rerun on new
  armies would show whether it is real.
- **Draws about halve with any hand** (40–60% fewer), and may fall a little as the hand grows
  (−0.8 ± 0.8 points a card, not clear; four cards read higher than three; six cards against three:
  −1.9 ± 2.6). This is the clean draw reading that measurement 1 could not give: here both sides
  are equally strong.
- **Games get shorter with every card**: −2.9 ± 1.4 turns a card; six cards against three: −8.9 ±
  4.3 turns.
- **Most cards get played** (share of held cards played, over the four hand sizes): Freeze,
  Flight and Sacrifice 91–96%, Ice Wall 84–91%, Haste 61–77%, Strike 60–69%, Leap 61–62%, March
  45–57%. Leap is played early (median ply 17), Haste and March late (median ply 46–56). The share
  falls a little as the hand grows: 80% of three cards, 74% of six.

## Salvation (built 2026-10-04, measured in Measurement 5)

Owner (2026-10-04): "'Salvation (a 2014 card)' - yes, build." Card text: **"Return one of your
captured pieces to an empty square of your back rank."** Card-only (`Salvation` in `CARD_ONLY`,
`card:Salvation` entrants, `--cardPool …,Salvation`); one use, and the card is the turn's move.

- **Which pieces:** the ones Sacrifice may bring back, from the same reserve (the pieces a side has
  lost, `Position.lost`, FEN field 7 `l…`): any piece but a pawn, a guard or the king. Not a pawn,
  because the card says "pieces", a pawn on its own first rank is not a chess position (no rule says
  how it moves from there), and it keeps the reserve one rule for both cards. Not a guard, as for
  Sacrifice: the guard is the measured draw engine, and the owner avoids rules that raise draws. A
  paladin that removed itself after a capture is in the reserve, as for Sacrifice.
- **Promoted pieces** return as what they were when taken (a captured promoted queen is a queen):
  the reserve records the piece, not its history, as for Sacrifice. A piece that returns leaves the
  reserve, so Sacrifice and Salvation in one hand draw on the same pieces.
- **Where:** any empty square of the player's own first rank (rank 1 for White, rank 8 for Black).
- **Check:** the move must not leave the own king in check; it may answer a check by blocking it,
  and it may give check (a rook on an open file, an archer's shot). That breaks no invariant: the
  returned piece attacks only as a piece on the board does, and no card move ever takes a king.
- **The returned piece** is new: unspent and unmarked (a Freeze mark on that square, if its piece
  was taken since, does not bind it). A spent card is gone. The move resets no 50-move clock.
- **Dead draws:** while a side holds an unplayed Salvation and a returnable piece waits, the game is
  not a material draw (conservative: any returnable piece).
- **Notation:** `N@b1!R` (a drop, `@`, as in crazyhouse, tagged `!R` for "return"); the replay and the
  tournament's use counter read it. The search counts an unplayed Salvation at half the best
  returnable piece's value (Sacrifice's share, not tuned for Salvation), so it plays the card soon
  after a loss.
- **Smoke** (6 games, `card:Salvation,none`, depth 2, outputs deleted): the card was played in 5 of
  6 games (first use: median ply 18; it returned a beast, a bishop and knights). The legality
  cross-check (fast path, full check and engine on random hands) includes it.

**Measurement 1 for Salvation** (`cards-a3`; not run, Measurement 5 replaced it): the card against no card on 300 colour-swapped
pairs, each on its own army, with the no-card mirror games on the same armies, as `cards-a2` (the
same flags; a new seed, because the pool changed to one beast on 2026-10-04, so `cards-a2`'s armies
cannot be replayed anyway). 1,200 games: about **5–6 min** with 16 workers on an idle Mac
(`cards-a2`: 3,000 games in 13.2 min), about 11 min on the 61 W charger.

```
nohup npx tsx src/sim/tournament.ts run --id cards-a3 --powers card:Salvation,none --anchor none --mirror --pairs 300 --depth 3 --seed 5656 --armies perPair --rule markFree=true --rule hasteCaptures=false --rule strikeCaptures=false --rule strikePawns=false --workers 16 > sim/out/cards-a3.log 2>&1 &
# when the log ends with "1200 games in …":
npx tsx src/sim/tournament.ts report --id cards-a3
```

## The 2014 cards (built 2026-10-04, measured in Measurement 5)

Owner (2026-10-04): "cards - let's add all and test variations for whatever might not work in our
version or might be overpowered." Every remaining card of the 2014 game and the rulebook drafts is
built as a card-only card (`CARD_ONLY`, a `card:<Name>` entrant, `--cardPool …,<Name>`). Where a card
has two readings, the softer or narrower one is a second card named `…B`, so a tournament plays each.
Each is one use; a side plays one card a turn. The texts are `cardText` in `src/powers-ui.ts`.

| card | text | notation |
|---|---|---|
| Rage | One of your pieces moves twice this turn and may take on either move (the second move is optional). | `Ra1-a4!A`, then a plain move or `--` |
| RageB | One of your pieces moves twice this turn; its second move, if it makes one, must take. | `Ra1-a4!B` |
| Mirror | Play the card your opponent played last, as if it were in your hand. | the copied move, then `!Y`: `!F:d5!Y` |
| MirrorB | Play another card from your hand; it stays in your hand. | the copied move, then `!Z` |
| Firewall | None of your pieces can be taken on your opponent's next turn; then make your move. | `!P` |
| FirewallB | Swap one of your pieces (not the king) with an enemy piece (not the king) next to it. | `!E:c4<>d5` |
| EarthQuake | Choose a square: each piece next to it, except a king, is pushed one square straight away from it if that square is empty. | `!Q:d4` |
| EarthQuakeB | The same, on a square next to one of your pieces. | `!U:d4` |
| Burn | One of your pieces (not a pawn or the king) takes an enemy piece on d4, e4, d5 or e5 as a queen would. | `Ra2xd5!N` |
| FireStarter | One of your pieces (not a pawn or the king) takes an enemy piece on the enemy back rank as a queen would. | `Ra1xh8!T` |
| Control | One of your pieces (not a pawn or the king) moves and takes this turn as a friendly piece next to it does. | `Nb1xb7!O` |
| Rescue | Your Freeze, Ice Wall or Firewall from your previous turn lasts one more turn; then make your move. | `!D:d6` (the mark's square) |
| Growth | As your move, draw the next card. | `!G` |
| GrowthB | Draw the next card, then make your move. | `!G+` |

**Readings decided in the build** (each one sentence of the card's rule; say if one should change):

- **Rage** is Haste's shape (the same follow-up move and `--`, the same piece) with captures on both
  moves under every Haste rule: `hasteCaptures: false` binds only Haste. The second move never takes
  a king; a paladin capture that would remove the paladin is not a first move (as for Haste).
- **Mirror** copies the opponent's most recent card, at any time in the game, with that card's rules
  (a Freeze is a free mark, a Haste a two-move turn). A Mirror records the card it played as, so a
  Mirror never copies a Mirror. **MirrorB** doubles any other unplayed card of the hand but a Mirror.
- **Firewall** is Ice Wall's mark on every piece of the side, with Ice Wall's timing (a free action
  under `markFree`, one opponent turn, two under `markTurns: 2`). It stops every capture of the side's
  pieces, a king's included, and every Curse and FirewallB on them. **Check is unchanged**: a
  Firewall changes no attacked square, so the opponent still gives check and checkmate, and the side
  under the Firewall may give check with a piece the opponent cannot take (it must move the king or
  block). Like any mark it changes no square, so it cannot answer a check. The king is never taken
  in this game anyway (checkmate ends it first).
- **FirewallB** never moves a king; a pawn does not land on the first or last rank (Curse's limit); a
  warded piece (Ice Wall, Firewall) is not swapped, as it is not cursed.
- **Earth Quake**: kings are not pushed (the Ogre never shoves one either). Any square may be the
  centre, empty or not; its own piece stays. The pushes are simultaneous; they cannot block each
  other. A pawn is not pushed onto the first or last rank; a guard only where a guard may stand. A
  frozen piece stays where it is (it does not move by any hand); a warded piece is pushed (a push
  takes nothing). A quake that exposes the own king is illegal. **EarthQuakeB**'s square must be next
  to one of the side's pieces or pawns, the king included.
- **Burn** and **Fire Starter**: the taker is a piece, not a pawn or the king (Strike's official
  reading; a pawn would land on the last rank for Fire Starter). The victim is any enemy piece or pawn
  but the king, if the taker may take it (`canCapture`: never a guard). A capture the piece makes by
  its own move is not offered again (Vault's rule). The taker lands on the square and keeps its type.
- **Control**: the mover is not a pawn or a king, and neither is the lender (a pawn's moves depend on
  its direction and promote; a king's on the king's power). The mover takes only what it may take
  itself (a guard borrows a rook's moves but takes nothing), keeps its type, and does not remove
  itself as a paladin would. It borrows shots and chains (a knight beside an archer shoots), never
  a swap or a shove.
- **Rescue is not buildable as worded.** "Only playable when such an effect is active": a Freeze,
  Ice Wall or Firewall binds only the opponent's next turn (`markTurns: 1`, the official reading),
  so on the side's own next turn it has already ended, and the card could never be played. **Built
  instead (nearest reading):** on the turn after the mark ends, the side may renew it, the same mark
  on the same square (every piece for a Firewall), for the opponent's next turn; the mark waits one
  turn as `left: 0` (FEN `md6w0`), binds nothing, and goes when the side's turn ends. Under
  `markTurns: 2` the mark is still live on the side's next turn and Rescue adds a turn. Rescue is a
  free action under `markFree`, as the marks are.
- **Growth**: the pile is the card pool in the pair's deal order, without the side's own hand: the
  same order for both sides, so a `cards<k>` mirror stays fair (`pileFor`). `card:Growth` draws from
  the default pool, the eight one-use powers. A drawn card is played later, never in the same turn;
  the hand holds at most 8 cards, dealt and drawn.
- **Material draw:** a side that may still play a Strike or a Salvation (held, copyable by its
  Mirror, or left in its Growth pile) keeps the game open, as before.

**State, for the lab:** `Position.rage` (FEN `hd4r` / `hd4t`), `Mark.all` (`me1wa`), a mark with
`left: 0` (`md6w0`), `Position.last` (`yHaste.Freeze`, kept while a hand holds a Mirror),
`Position.drawn` (`d1.0`) and `Rules.piles` (`--rule piles=Haste+Freeze`). All are in the search's
key (new keys appended after every earlier draw, so stored games replay), in FEN and in the replay.

**Search values of an unplayed card** (guesses, not measured, as for the other card-only cards):
Rage 180, RageB 150 (Haste 150), Firewall 60 (Ice Wall 30), FirewallB 40, Earth Quake 40, EarthQuakeB
30, Burn 30, Fire Starter 60, Control 60, Rescue 20, Growth 30, GrowthB 50 centipawns. A Mirror holds

**Not playable against no card:** `card:Mirror` against `none` never plays (the opponent plays no

**Checks** (2026-10-04): unit tests per card (`src/rules/cards-2014.test.ts`: what each allows and

## Measurement 5: every card against no card (run 2026-10-04)

Each new card against no card, as measurements 1 and 3: 300 colour-swapped pairs per entrant, each
pair on its own army, with the no-card mirror games on the same armies, depth 3, the card rules of
`cards-a2`. Entrants (15 and `none`): the 13 new cards that can act against no card, Rescue and
MirrorB each held with a Freeze, the Freeze alone for their difference, and Salvation (so `cards-a3`
is not needed). Mirror plays in its own round against a Freeze (5b). New seed 5858.

- **cards-m5:** 16 × 600 = **9,600 games**. On the idle Mac with 16 workers (`cards-a2`'s rate, 227
  games a minute) about **42 min**; about twice that on the 61 W charger. With the M1 and five Kaggle
  notebooks, in 23 shards: the Mac plays 0–15 (one worker each), the M1 16–17 (four workers each),
  Kaggle 18–22 (one notebook each, about 1/15 of the Mac): about **30–37 min**, Kaggle last. The M1's
  speed on mains power is not measured; if it is slow, its two shards are the tail.
- **cards-m5-mirror (5b):** `card:Freeze+Mirror` and `card:Mirror` against `card:Freeze`, with its
  mirror games: 3 × 600 = **1,800 games**, about **8 min** on the Mac alone after cards-m5.
- Reading: each entrant's value and draws against `none` (the report's anchor section); Rescue's and
  MirrorB's worth are their rows minus `card:Freeze`'s; Mirror's is its row in 5b against the Freeze.
  A card far above Haste (+2.3 pawns, measurement 1) is the overpowered one to trim, and an A/B pair
  (Rage and RageB, …) says whether the softer reading is enough.

Push the commit first (Kaggle and the M1 play a pushed commit). Every machine runs the same flags:

## Recommendation

- **Hand size: eight** for the fewest draws and the shortest games (measurement 4), as fair as six;
  **six** if eight cards are too many for a player to keep in mind (the owner's first idea; as fair,
  2.6 points more draws, 7 turns longer). Deal from all twelve cards (measurement 4). These numbers describe
  the computer player at depth 3; people may play cards differently.
- **The pool:** March and Ice Wall are the weakest cards (+0.5 and +0.6 pawns; the measurement
  cannot separate them). March is also played least (about half the time), so it is the first
  candidate to replace with a new card.
- **Next:** build the new cards the owner picks, measure each against no card as in measurement
  1, then a same-hand round with the larger pool. Card mode is not in the playable game yet.

pick from. All are built now (Mimic, Vault, Curse and Sky Lift on 2026-10-03, the rest on
2026-10-04, sections above); each needs its own measurement. The build ratings come from a
read-only review of the engine with an adversarial check (2026-10-03). Small: under about 100

## Method

- Depth 3 search, the linear evaluation, four random opening plies, a fresh army for every pair
  (`--armies perPair`). Intervals are 95%, over armies. Rules: the official readings of the
  spendable powers (Freeze and Ice Wall a free action, Haste without captures, Strike pieces only
  without captures), each card one use.
- A game ends when one side leads by 6 pawns for 3 plies in a row (resign; 89.5% of the 4,000
  same-hand games, 91.0% of those with cards), or by the usual draws, including a quiet-position draw after ply 68.
- Pawns: score converted to Elo, then at the balance lab's 64 Elo per pawn (depth 3, measured
  2026-09-13 with ±25% of its own), so a pawn value carries that uncertainty too.
- Compute: the Mac and up to 4 Kaggle notebooks at once (the account allows 5), sharing one
  commit per run; Kaggle games are identical to the Mac's move for move.
- Tools: `src/sim/tournament.ts` (`--anchor`, `cards<k>`, `--mirrorOnly`), `tools/kaggle-tournament.mjs`.
- Raw games: branch `claude/kp2-results-r13-cards`; reports and specs in `sim/out/`.
