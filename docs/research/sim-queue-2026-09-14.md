# The queue of 2026-09-14 — five questions answered (Q1–Q5)

Saar launched `docs/QUEUE.md` as one chain on 2026-09-14. Five questions ran, 41 600 games, depth 3.
Q6 (the AI pass) is still running and is not in this report.

---

## 0. The short version, in plain words

**The guard's double step from home does not pay.** Saar's idea was a pawn-style double step: the
guard leaves its home square and travels two squares once, then never again. The point was to make
the guard do more without dragging the endgame, because a rule that only fires on move one cannot
touch the endgame. It does not work out that way. The `slide` reading (through an empty middle
square) moves no metric outside its error bar — it is free, and it buys nothing. The `leap` reading
(over the pawn in front) does move numbers, and the wrong ones: games run 5 plies longer and hit the
ply cap twice as often. **Both readings drag draws exactly as hard as the two-square Warden Saar
already rejected** — a guard sits next to its own king at the end of 51–52% of drawn games, against
40.5% for today's Wall. **Keep the Wall.**

**The two buff candidates are free, and they are Saar's to take on taste.** `paladinKamikaze=nonPawn`
(the paladin survives a pawn capture) moves nothing at all: balance, decisive games, draws and game
length are all inside their bars. `maesterSwapAny` (swap with any friend, not just an adjacent one)
also leaves balance and draws alone. It does two visible things: it shortens games by about 4 plies
and adds 6 legal moves to every position, and kings sit still much more often — one game in four ends
with a king that never moved, against one in six today. Both pieces already priced closer to a knight
in the earlier value runs (paladin 2.87 pawns, maester 3.1, against a knight's 2.96). **So both buffs
raise the piece's worth and cost the game nothing. Neither is a balance fix, because nothing is
broken; they are identity calls.**

**The paladin's White edge is the piece, not its two special rules.** White scores about 0.55 on a
board with a paladin. Two diagnostics have now switched off one of the paladin's clauses each: taking
away the jump over friends left the edge alone (earlier pass), and taking away the self-sacrifice
leaves it alone too — if anything it rises, 0.554 → 0.586. So the edge is not the jump and not the
first favourable trade. **It is simply a queen-ranged piece on an open board, and White moves first.**

**Black's double first turn over-corrects. Do not adopt it.** Letting Black play two moves on its
first turn takes White's score from 0.533 to 0.472 on the full pool — it hands Black an edge about
the size of the one White had. On a pool with no paladin it lands near even (0.526 → 0.490). Nothing
else changes: draws, decisive games and game length all stay put. The rule is a large, blunt
correction for a small, accepted tilt, and it costs the game a first turn that is not chess.

**One number in the first pass was a measuring fault, not a result.** Under the double-turn rule the
analyzer's "killer move" and "interest" columns collapsed by 0.21 and 0.04. That was the analyzer
reading the wrong player, not the rule making the game dull. §5.2 explains it; it was fixed the same
day, the two reports were regenerated without replaying a game, and those rows now read as noise
(§7). Every row in this document is sound.

## 1. Method

Every run below is a **paired A/B**: two whole populations play themselves over the **same
arrangements** and the **same opening seeds**, so the openings cancel. There is no match and no SPRT,
because a symmetric rule changes both sides at once. Read the **mean paired difference over
arrangements** with a 95% normal approximation. Depth 3, `timeMs: Infinity`, 4 random opening plies,
ply cap 300, Fishtest adjudication (`SIM-PLAN.md` §2).

**Bold** marks a row that clears its own interval. With one interval per metric and 40–60
arrangements, read "clears" as *worth a second run*, not as a test (`SIM-PLAN.md` §3).

| run | control | games an arm | arrangements | pool |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ab-guard-dbl-slide` | `ab-warden-wall` | 1 500 | 60 (all one-guard) | shipped |
| `ab-guard-dbl-leap` | `ab-warden-wall` | 1 500 | 60 (all one-guard) | shipped |
| `pb-ab-L-nonPawn` | `pb-ab-base24` | 1 600 | 40 | shipped |
| `pb-ab-M-swapAny` | `pb-ab-base24` | 1 600 | 40 | shipped |
| `pb-lp-never` | `pb-lp-base` | 800 | 40 | `QRRBBNNL` |
| `dt-full` | `dt-full-base` | 4 000 | 40 | shipped |
| `dt-nopal` | `dt-nopal-base` | 4 000 | 40 | `QRRBBNNAAGMMSS` |

Guard activity, drag and the pool check come from `tools/warden-report.ts`, which reads every rule
**off the moves** and never off the stored spec (`LESSONS.md`, 2026-09-13).

## 2. Q1 — the guard's double step from the home rank

The guard keeps its identity in both arms: **immortal** (only a king removes it) and it **captures
nothing**. `guardDoubleFirst` gives it one double step, from its home square only.

| reading | what it does |
|---|---|
| **`slide`** | two squares along a line, the middle square must be empty — the pawn in front blocks it until that pawn moves |
| **`leap`** | two squares along a line, over anything — it reaches rank 3 on move one |
| **Wall** (today) | one square, every move |

### 2.1 The paired A/B against the Wall

Wall column pooled over its 1 500 games; each Δ is the mean paired difference over the same 60 ranks.

| metric | Wall | `slide` Δ | `leap` Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| white score | 0.532 | −0.018 ± 0.022 | **−0.036 ± 0.025** |
| decisive share | 0.702 | −0.019 ± 0.023 | −0.023 ± 0.033 |
| draw share | 0.283 | +0.013 ± 0.022 | +0.006 ± 0.033 |
| capped (ply cap 300) | 0.015 | +0.005 ± 0.009 | **+0.017 ± 0.010** |
| dead-material endings | 2.2% | 2.6% | 2.5% |
| mean plies | 121.7 | +2.7 ± 2.8 | **+5.2 ± 3.9** |
| branching factor | 31.8 | **+0.7 ± 0.2** | **+1.5 ± 0.3** |
| min utilisation | 0.45 | −0.00 ± 0.01 | **−0.02 ± 0.01** |
| interest (resid.) | −0.001 | +0.002 ± 0.003 | −0.001 ± 0.004 |

**`slide` clears nothing but branching** — it is a rule the engine can rarely play, and the numbers
say so. **`leap` clears four rows and every one of them is a cost**: the ply cap more than doubles
(1.5% → 3.3% of games), games run 5 plies longer, and the least-used piece is used less. The white
score row is not a balance finding — the rule is symmetric, so a paired shift of −0.036 on 60 ranks
reads as noise plus a slower game, not as an edge for Black.

**Every one of these 60 ranks holds exactly one guard** (confirmed from the moves, §2.3). A guard
reaches only about 48% of dealt armies, so **multiply every difference above by 0.479** for the game
as it is dealt.

### 2.2 Guard activity — the rules are confirmed from the moves

Per **side-game that started with a guard** (3 000 an arm). The `2-step` column is the new move, so it
separates the arms; the Wall reads 0.00 as it must.

| arm | moves/game | 2-step/game | squares seen | adj. king (plies) | shields a king ray (games) | denies a promo square (games) | survival | taken by a king |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wall | 4.43 | 0.00 | 3.53 | 33.2% | 16.2% | 74.7% | 95.2% | 145 |
| `slide` | 5.49 | 1.17 | 4.06 | 36.9% | 19.7% | 73.9% | 95.6% | 131 |
| `leap` | **6.18** | **1.51** | **4.45** | **37.5%** | **23.2%** | 66.4% | 96.2% | 113 |

*Counting both armies, that is 8.9 → 11.0 guard moves a game for `slide` and 12.4 for `leap` — the
per-piece table in each `.experiment.md` uses that denominator.*

**The guard does play more under both readings**, and `leap` most of all: a quarter more moves than
`slide`, and it shields its king's line in 23.2% of games against the Wall's 16.2%. The one activity
column that falls is *denies a promotion square* (74.7% → 66.4%), which is the endgame job — a guard
that spent its double step early is not better placed late.

### 2.3 Guard drag — the number that decides it

Share of **drawn** side-games that end with a guard among the side's last 3 non-king pieces, or next
to its own king. This is the pattern Saar rejected the Warden for.

| arm | drawn side-games | among the last 3 | next to its king | either |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wall | 894 | 10.1% | 32.3% | 40.5% |
| `slide` | 950 | 13.5% | 40.0% | **51.5%** |
| `leap` | 964 | 13.1% | 41.1% | **51.9%** |
| *Warden A (rejected)* | *976* | *15.8%* | *39.0%* | *52.0%* |

**This is the finding.** The rejected two-square Warden dragged 52.0%. **Both double-step readings
drag 51.5% and 51.9% — the same number.** The "it only fires on move one, so it cannot touch the
endgame" argument fails in practice: a guard that starts its life two squares out arrives at the
endgame on a different square and stays near its king exactly as often.

### 2.4 End reasons

| arm | adjudicated draw | adjudicated resign | draw50 | dead material | repetition | ply cap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wall | 24.1% | 70.2% | 1.3% | 0.9% | 1.9% | 1.5% |
| `slide` | 25.1% | 68.3% | 1.5% | 1.1% | 1.9% | 2.1% |
| `leap` | 24.8% | 67.9% | 1.5% | 0.9% | 1.6% | **3.3%** |

### 2.5 Verdict — keep the Wall

**Reject both readings. `guardDoubleFirst` stays a lab toggle.**

The decision bar in `QUEUE.md` asked for three things at once: the decisive share must not fall, the
capped and dead-ending shares must not rise, and guard activity must rise. **`leap` fails the second
and passes the third; `slide` passes the second and the third is not worth having**, because
everything else about the game is unchanged and the drag is up 11 points. Saar's stated priority is
fewer draws and fewer stuck games. On that priority the Wall wins both comparisons.

## 3. Q2 and Q3 — the paladin and maester buffs

**Read §3.1 before the tables.** The first pass of these two A/Bs was void.

### 3.1 The void control, and why the first pass showed miracles

The queue told both A/Bs to resume the old control `pb-ab-base`. That control is **not a control for
this pool**, and the numbers prove it from the files:

| | `pb-ab-base` (void) | `pb-ab-base24` (clean) | the two arms |
|---|---|---|---|
| games | 1 600 | 1 600 | 1 600 each |
| distinct arrangements | **79** | 40 | 40 |
| games on the 40 arrangements the arms played | **94** | 1 600 | 1 600 |
| games whose back rank holds two guards | **384** (10 ranks) | 0 | 0 |
| capped share | **7.1%** | 1.0% | 0.9% |
| mean plies | **139.3** | 117.3 | 117.1 |

`pb-ab-base` was played 1 544 games under the **two-guard** pool and then resumed for 56 games under
the one-guard pool. A "paired" A/B against it pairs almost nothing: **94 of its 1 600 games** fall on
the arrangements the arms actually played. Its capped share of 7.1% is its own two-guard games. Read
against it, both buffs looked like cures — **capped 7.1% → 0.9%** — and neither buff touches the ply
cap at all.

This is the lesson already in `LESSONS.md` ("A resumed control run silently mixes pools", 2026-09-14):
**the runner records the rule *diff* against the defaults of the day, so "all defaults" is not a fixed
thing, and an old control must never be resumed after `POOL` or `DEFAULT_RULES` changes.** The chain
then played a clean control, `pb-ab-base24`, on today's pool and re-ran both A/Bs against it. The
`.experiment.md` files now name `pb-ab-base24`, and its arrangement set is identical to both arms'.

`pb-ab-base` stays on disk. It is the evidence behind the lesson, and older documents cite it by
name. **It is void as a control for any new comparison.**

### 3.2 The clean result

Paired differences over the same 40 arrangements, against `pb-ab-base24`.

| metric | base | `paladinKamikaze=nonPawn` Δ | `maesterSwapAny` Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| white score | 0.546 | −0.003 ± 0.018 | −0.010 ± 0.030 |
| decisive share | 0.721 | −0.008 ± 0.022 | +0.005 ± 0.031 |
| draw share | 0.269 | +0.009 ± 0.022 | −0.004 ± 0.030 |
| capped | 0.010 | −0.001 ± 0.003 | −0.001 ± 0.006 |
| dead-material endings | 1.1% | 1.3% | 1.0% |
| mean plies | 117.3 | −0.3 ± 1.3 | **−4.3 ± 3.4** |
| branching factor | 33.1 | −0.2 ± 0.2 | **+6.1 ± 1.3** |
| games where a king never moved | 16.6% | 17.6% | **25.4%** |
| maester swaps a game | 7.07 | 7.13 | **13.05** |
| paladin sacrifices a game | 0.68 | 0.59 | 0.69 |
| interest (resid.) | −0.000 | +0.001 ± 0.003 | +0.001 ± 0.003 |

**`paladinKamikaze=nonPawn` is a null result on every axis that matters.** Balance, decisiveness,
draws, length, branching: all inside their intervals, and the ones that are not (the residual interest
terms) move by less than a hundredth. The piece itself changes — sacrifices fall from 0.68 to 0.59 a
game, because pawn captures no longer cost it its life — and the game does not notice.

**`maesterSwapAny` leaves balance and draws alone and changes the texture.** Swaps nearly double
(7.07 → 13.05 a game), every position gains 6 legal moves, games shorten by 4 plies, and the king
stands still far more often: **a king never moves in 25.4% of games, against 16.6%**. That last row is
the one to look at, and it has two readings — a maester that can rescue any friend from anywhere means
the king does not have to walk, which is either a good castling-like safety valve or a king that
stopped playing. The maester's own survival falls (33.0% → 25.7%): it is doing more and dying for it.
**No swap loop appeared**: the repetition, 50-move and ply-cap shares are all flat or down.

### 3.3 With the value arms: both buffs are free upgrades

`sim-lm-buffs-2026-09-13.md` §2 priced these two candidates against a knight (2.96 pawns) in a
300-game odds match:

| candidate | value against a knight | A/B here |
|---|---|---|
| today's paladin | 2.21 ± 0.52 pawns | — |
| **`paladinKamikaze=nonPawn`** | **2.87 ± 0.57** (0.13 under a knight) | nothing moves |
| today's maester | 2.18 ± 0.53 pawns | — |
| **`maesterSwapAny`** | **3.12 ± 0.57** (0.12 over a knight) | balance and draws flat |

**Both buffs raise the piece's worth by about 0.7–0.9 pawns and move none of the game's numbers.**
That is the cleanest shape a buff can have: the piece becomes worth playing, and the game stays the
game. Neither is a balance fix, because neither the paladin nor the maester is out of balance.
**These are identity calls, and they are Saar's**: §6 of `sim-lm-buffs` argues both keep the piece's
printed identity intact (the paladin still buys everything that matters with its life; the swap is
still the maester's verb).

## 4. Q5 — where White's paladin edge comes from

Pool `QRRBBNNL`, so almost every rank carries a paladin. `promotionSet=anyNonKing` is pinned on
**both** arms, because the control predates the guard-promotion ban. 800 games an arm, 40
arrangements.

| metric | today's paladin | `paladinKamikaze=never` | paired Δ ±95% |
|---|---|---|---|
| white score | 0.554 | 0.586 | +0.032 ± 0.035 |
| decisive share | 0.846 | 0.907 | **+0.061 ± 0.030** |
| draw share | 0.149 | 0.092 | **−0.056 ± 0.029** |
| capped | 0.005 | 0.000 | −0.005 ± 0.006 |
| mean plies | 90.8 | 82.8 | **−8.0 ± 2.4** |
| branching factor | 31.4 | 30.7 | **−0.7 ± 0.3** |
| paladin sacrifices a game | 0.95 | 0.00 | (the rule) |
| games where a king never moved | 21.9% | 30.0% | +8.1 |

**The diagnostic answers its question by not moving.** The hypothesis under test was that White's
edge comes from the first favourable kamikaze trade, which White gets to take first. If that were so,
switching the sacrifice off would pull the edge down toward the paladin-free 0.510. **It goes up
instead: 0.554 → 0.586.** Put beside `sim-lm-buffs` §4, where removing the *jump* left the edge at
0.556 (paired −0.004 ± 0.045), both of the paladin's special clauses are now ruled out.

**So the paladin's White edge is neither the jump nor the sacrifice trade. It is the piece's reach on
an open board — a queen-ranged attacker in a pool with no other long-range fairy piece — and White
moves first with it.**

A paladin that never dies also sharpens the game hard: 6 more decisive games in 100, 5.6 fewer draws,
8 plies shorter. That is a second queen, and `sim-lm-buffs` §6 calls it an identity change
(the sacrifice is the piece's whole bargain). It is not a recommendation here; it is the control arm
of a diagnostic.

**The honest limit.** ±0.035 on the paired white-score row, and `sim-lm-buffs` §4 quotes **±0.045**
for the same measurement on the paladin-carrying subset. Either bar rejects a full 0.05 fall. **Neither
resolves a 0.03 shift**, which is roughly the size of the movement seen. Answering "how much of the
edge is which clause" to ±0.009 needs about 10 000 games an arm — about four hours a cell.

## 5. Q4 — Black's double first turn (`RUNS.md` R9)

`secondPlayerDoubleFirstTurn`: Black's first turn is two moves, and the first of them may not give
check. 4 000 games an arm, 40 arrangements, two pools.

### 5.1 The result

| metric | full pool base | `dt-full` Δ | no-paladin base | `dt-nopal` Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **white score** | **0.533** | **−0.062 ± 0.026** | **0.526** | **−0.036 ± 0.022** |
| decisive share | 0.739 | +0.011 ± 0.019 | 0.705 | +0.013 ± 0.025 |
| draw share | 0.254 | −0.014 ± 0.018 | 0.282 | −0.011 ± 0.025 |
| capped | 0.008 | **+0.004 ± 0.003** | 0.013 | −0.003 ± 0.004 |
| mean plies | 114.4 | +0.1 ± 2.0 | 119.6 | −1.5 ± 1.6 |
| branching factor | 33.0 | +0.0 ± 0.2 | 30.9 | +0.1 ± 0.1 |
| dead-material endings | 1.5% | 1.4% | 1.7% | 1.4% |

**On the full pool the rule over-compensates.** White 0.533 → **0.472**: the rule takes an edge of
+0.033 away from White and hands Black one of +0.028. That is a mirror image, not a correction, and
it clears its interval by more than a factor of two. **Without the paladin it lands near even**: 0.526
→ **0.490**, Black +0.010, inside a whisker of fair. Nothing else in the game moves in either pool —
draws, decisiveness and length are all flat.

**The two controls also price the paladin again.** White scores 0.533 with the paladin in the pool and
0.526 without it (unpaired, different pools, so this is a consistency check and not a measurement).
A paladin reaches about 47% of dealt armies, so a 0.007 gap across the pool is about **+0.015 of
White's score per paladin dealt** — consistent with the +0.02 from `sim-configs-2026-09-13.md` and
batch 3.

### 5.2 Two rows in the source tables were a measuring fault (fixed 2026-09-14)

The first `.experiment.md` pages for both runs showed **killer move −0.209 ± 0.009** (`dt-full`) and
**−0.211 ± 0.013** (`dt-nopal`), and **interest −0.041** / **−0.042**. Those numbers were wrong.
`leadMetrics()` in `src/sim/analyze.ts` identified the mover by **ply parity**
(`idx[i] % 2 === 0 ? 1 : -1`). Black's double turn puts two Black moves in a row at the start, so from
the third move on the parity was inverted for the rest of the game: the metric measured the largest
swing *against* the mover, then clamped it at zero. `interest` is a weighted sum that includes
`killerMove`, so it inherited the fault. The near-identical size in two different pools was the
signature of a systematic fault rather than an effect.

**Fixed the same day.** `moverAt(ply, rules)` in `src/rules/engine.ts` is now the one place that
answers "who moved at this index", and every reader calls it (`LESSONS.md`, "A metric that reads
the mover off ply parity"). Both pages were rebuilt from the stored games with
`--experiment ab --noReplay`, without playing a game:

| metric | full pool base | `dt-full` Δ | no-paladin base | `dt-nopal` Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| killer move | 0.326 | +0.010 ± 0.010 | 0.335 | +0.007 ± 0.009 |
| interest | 0.480 | +0.003 ± 0.003 | 0.484 | +0.002 ± 0.003 |

**Read: the rule does not change the game's texture.** Killer move sits a hair over its bar on the
full pool and inside it on the no-paladin pool; interest is inside its bar on both. Every
parity-free row — score, decisive, draws, capped, plies, branching, lead change, uncertainty, drama,
permanence — never depended on the mover and did not move on the re-read. The white-score row is
unchanged (0.533 → 0.472, 0.526 → 0.490), so §5.4 stands on it alone.

### 5.3 The chess background

In classical chess White scores about **54–55% at master level**, and the game accepts the tilt: it is
part of what makes a game of chess a pair of games, one with each colour. **A double first move for
Black is not a remedy chess practice uses.** The compensation devices people do use are komi-style —
the **pie rule** (one player divides, the other chooses) and **Armageddon** (Black gets draw odds in
exchange for less time). Both leave the moves of the game untouched and put the compensation in the
*conditions*. If Saar ever wants an equaliser for King Down, that is the family worth looking at, not
an extra move.

### 5.4 Verdict — do not adopt

**Do not adopt `secondPlayerDoubleFirstTurn`.** It over-corrects on the pool the game ships with, it
costs Black's first turn its identity as a chess turn, and the tilt it corrects is one that chess has
accepted for a century. R9 is answered.

## 7. Limits

- **Depth 3, one depth.** Nothing here is confirmed at a second depth, which `SIM-PLAN.md` §9 asks for
  before a rule ships. The strength ladder (`sim-strength-2026-09-13.md`) found balance conclusions
  depth-stable and draw, interest and per-rank rankings **not** depth-stable — so the Q4 balance
  answer and the two null results carry over better than the Q1 draw and pace numbers do.
- **One rank set per question.** Q1 uses the Warden's 60 ranks, Q2–Q5 use 40 each. A second rank set
  was not run.
- **The Q1 A/B conditions on a guard being dealt.** Multiply by 0.479 for the game as dealt.
- **Q5 resolves ±0.035 (paired) and is quoted at ±0.045 on the paladin subset.** It rejects a full
  0.05 fall and cannot resolve a 0.03 one.
- **The engine prices a buffed piece at its old value.** `src/ai/eval.ts` is fitted to today's rules,
  so a search that owns a buffed paladin or maester trades it too cheaply. Every buff row is a **lower
  bound** on the buff.
- **The double-turn measurement is a lower bound on the compensation.** `src/ai/search.ts` alternates
  colours inside its own tree and cannot search the double move, and the default 4 random opening
  plies mean the double falls inside the random opening. A *chosen* extra move is worth more than a
  random one, so a real double first turn over-compensates by at least as much as measured — which
  strengthens the rejection, not weakens it.
- **`killerMove` and `interest` under `secondPlayerDoubleFirstTurn`** (§5.2): the mover-parity fault is fixed (`moverAt` in `src/rules/engine.ts`, 2026-09-14) and both `dt-*` reports were regenerated without replay; the corrected rows are +0.010 ± 0.010 and +0.003 ± 0.003 (dt-full), +0.007 ± 0.009 and +0.002 ± 0.003 (dt-nopal) — noise. The balance row, and the verdict, are unchanged.
- **`pb-ab-base` is void as a control** (§3.1). Runs before 2026-09-14 that were read against it under
  the two-guard pool are unaffected; nothing new may use it.
