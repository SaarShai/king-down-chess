# Queen presence: controlled pool comparison (2026-09-17)

The shipped pool `QLRRBBNNAAGMMSS` holds **one** `Q` among 15 letters. `sampleBackRank`
(`src/sim/spec.ts:165-174`) draws **7 letters plus the king**, so the queen starts on a rank in
7/15 = 46.7% of games before the opposite-colour-bishop rejection, and the sampled rank is mirrored,
so the queen is in both armies or neither. A census over `sim/out/pb-ab-G-nocap.base.jsonl` and
`sim/out/pb-ab-A-shots.base.jsonl` (3,200 games; recomputed below) found White scoring 56.4% on
queen ranks against 52.7% otherwise, a +3.67-point shift (95% CI [+0.2, +7.2]). That contrast is
observational: the queen share of the rank comes with five or six other letters, and the two census
files carry different stamps. This note asks the same question with a controlled paired comparison:
the shipped pool against the same pool minus the queen, everything else identical. The question is
"queen + 6 others" against "7 others" — what the queen contributes to the game.

## Method

- **Specs.** `sim/specs/queen-2026-09-17/with.json` and `without.json`. Both carry `games: 2000`,
  `backRanks: { sample: 40 }`, `ai: { depth: 3 }`, `seed: 88`, `commonSeeds: true`. `with` uses the
  shipped pool `QLRRBBNNAAGMMSS` (15 letters, one `Q`); `without` uses `LRRBBNNAAGMMSS` — the same
  pool with the one `Q` removed (14 letters) — and is otherwise identical. `loadSpec` reads the JSON
  `backRanks` object (`src/sim/spec.ts:208-214`), `buildJobs` consumes it (`src/sim/spec.ts:267-274`),
  and `sampleBackRank` draws 7 letters plus the king (`src/sim/spec.ts:165-174`). The spec files are
  the whole experiment; no CLI flags were needed.
- **Runs.** Sequential from the repo root, 4 workers on a shared machine:

  ```
  node_modules/.bin/tsx src/sim/run.ts --spec sim/specs/queen-2026-09-17/with.json --workers 4
  node_modules/.bin/tsx src/sim/run.ts --spec sim/specs/queen-2026-09-17/without.json --workers 4
  ```

  `with`: 2,000 games in 777.8 s (2.6 games/s) -> `sim/out/queen-with.jsonl` +
  `queen-with.summary.json`. `without`: 2,000 games in 831.1 s (2.4 games/s) ->
  `sim/out/queen-without.jsonl` + `queen-without.summary.json`. Each file holds 2,000 complete
  lines with no malformed ranks. Both runs logged `rules {}, values {}`, so both play the shipped
  defaults under the same rules key (`60dd023b`) and source (`418c0d5c5318`).
- **What the pools sample.** `sampleBackRank` draws 7 letters, so the queen is picked with
  probability 7/15. The `with` sample realized **17 ranks with the queen and 23 without**, so 850 of
  2,000 games (42.5%) start with the queen in each army and 1,150 (57.5%) do not; the `without`
  sample realized 0 ranks with a `Q` (0 of 2,000 games). The arms compare the shipped pool with the
  same pool minus its one queen, not a fixed one-versus-zero queen count.
- **Pairing.** `commonSeeds: true` gives game *i* the same per-game RNG seed in both files
  (2,000/2,000 match on `gameId` 0-1,999, with 0 seed mismatches and 0 opening-ply mismatches). The
  pools differ, so **0 of 2,000 games share a back rank** (the 40 ranks of the two arms are
  disjoint). Of the 2,000 pairs, 850 have the queen in the `with` arm only, 1,150 have no queen in
  either arm, and none have a queen in the `without` arm.
- **Analysis.** A throwaway Node script read both JSONL files. The paired interval is
  1.96 · sd(per-game Δ) / √n over the common `gameId`s; arm rates use Wilson intervals; decisiveness
  discordance uses McNemar with continuity correction. The intention-to-treat (ITT) estimate uses
  all 2,000 pairs; the per-protocol estimate uses the 850 pairs where the queen is actually on a
  rank, and the remaining 1,150 pairs act as a placebo (both pools then draw 7 of the same 14
  non-queen letters, so their expected difference is zero).

## Pooled result

| arm | pool | games | decisive | draw rate | white score | mean plies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| with | `QLRRBBNNAAGMMSS` | 2,000 | 0.8200 (1,640) | 0.1800 (360) | 0.55400 (928 W / 360 D / 712 B) | 101.64 |
| without | `LRRBBNNAAGMMSS` | 2,000 | 0.8190 (1,638) | 0.1810 (362) | 0.54100 (901 W / 362 D / 737 B) | 104.79 |
| with − without | | | +0.0010 | −0.0010 | +0.01300 | −3.15 |

Decisive share is 82.0% (Wilson 95% CI 80.3-83.6) with the queen in the pool and 81.9% (80.2-83.5)
without; the draw rate is 18.0% (16.4-19.7) against 18.1% (16.5-19.8). The pooled point estimates
put White 1.30 points higher (+0.01300, unpaired 95% CI [−0.0088, +0.0348]) and games 3.15 plies
shorter (−3.1485, unpaired 95% CI [−6.143, −0.154]) when the queen is in the pool; the unpaired
decisive interval is [−0.0228, +0.0248] and the draw interval mirrors it, so plies is the one
unpaired interval that excludes zero. Draw reasons are flat: adjudicated draws 278 against 267,
repetition 46 against 52, ply caps 15 against 13, fifty-move 11 against 18, insufficient material 9
against 11, stalemate 1 against 1.

The placebo subset is flat on all four metrics (largest point estimate +1.8 plies, every interval

(0.425 · 2.706 + 0.575 · 0.261 = 1.300 points of White score). McNemar on decisiveness counts 295
draws that only occur with the queen and 297 decisive games that only occur with the queen (z = +0.04,
p = 0.97); 65 games drew in both arms, and those are part of the 752 score ties.

Within the `with` arm, ranks that drew the queen are 2.1 points more decisive and 7.7 plies shorter
than ranks that did not, and their White score is +0.1 point. This is rank-level selection, not the
controlled removal — a queen rank spends one of its 7 picks on the queen — but the pace contrast is
the same direction as the paired result, and the fairness contrast is essentially zero.

## Verdict

- **Decisiveness and draws: no measurable effect.** The queen changes the decisive share by +0.10
  points (paired 95% CI [−2.28, +2.48]) and the draw rate by −0.10 points ([−2.48, +2.28]); McNemar
  p = 0.97. The census's +4.04-point decisiveness gap does not reproduce.
- **Fairness: the census's White shift does not reproduce at its full size, and this run does not
  establish it.** The ITT estimate is +1.30 points of White score (95% CI [−1.48, +4.08]). Among the
  850 pairs where the queen is actually on a rank it is +2.71 points (95% CI [−1.66, +7.07]). Both
  estimates lean the same way as the census, and the census's +3.67 points sits inside the ITT
  interval, so the two are compatible; but neither controlled interval excludes zero, and the
  queen-present point estimate is about three-quarters of the census value, not the full census
  shift. The ITT interval reaches +4.08 points, so an effect of the census's size fits inside it;
  this design cannot exclude it, only fail to confirm it.
- **Pace: the queen shortens games.** The ITT estimate is −3.15 plies (95% CI [−6.18, −0.12]); among
  pairs where the queen is actually present it is **−9.84 plies (95% CI [−14.60, −5.07])**, about 9%
  of the no-queen mean of 104.8. The census's observational pace gap (−6.8 plies) points the same way.
- **Bottom line.** Replacing one non-queen pick with the queen leaves decisiveness and the draw rate
  unchanged at this resolution and makes games clearly shorter when the queen is on a rank. It moves
  White score in the expected positive direction, but the controlled estimate is smaller than the
  census's and not statistically resolved; at 850 queen-present pairs the per-protocol White-score
  interval still spans roughly ±4.4 points around the estimate.
