The Visual Grammar

One mark language for every piece, power, card and rule. A fill moves. A target takes. A target with an arrow takes from afar.

I

The three tiles

The owner's own legend, laid on the game's stone. Shape carries the meaning; colour only repeats it.

Taking from afar, and refused

II

Every mark, every size

One renderer draws each mark on the board, on a phone, on a card diagram, on a thumbnail and as a ledge glyph. Each one reads on light and dark stone.

III

Lines, leaps and hops

A line is a rail of green tiles. How it ends tells why it stops.

IV

Only sometimes

A stitched frame means "conditional", and nothing else is ever dashed. Full when awake. Hollow when asleep.

V

Zones in chalk

Where a rule works. Chalk never means an action. It shows only while its rule is in focus.

VI

Other effects

Push, swap, becomes, kings' powers, LAB cards, frost and threats. Each has one colour and one shape.

VII

Seals and sigils

A wax seal is what a rule does. Its sigil is its name: the same sigil marks the squares it changes.

More sigils

VIII

Impressions

Which rule made which mark. An impression sits only where a rule changes the result.

IX

When chips and pills

Three shapes, three meanings.

X

Combinations

Knots join two seals. Gold: they shape one square together. Cracked: one stops the other. A grey "?" means not checked.

XI

A king carries two coins. Diamonds count uses. A LAB card has one use and a burgundy ribbon.
