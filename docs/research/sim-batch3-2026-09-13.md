# Batch 3 — the mining hypotheses, measured (2026-09-13)

Batch 3 of `docs/RUNS.md`, re-based onto the v0.6 rules and the tuned evaluation, plus the depth-4 interest check. **32 runs,
76 100 games, 2 h 16 m of engine time.** Every run plays depth 3 unless its row says otherwise, with `commonSeeds: true`, 20
back ranks an arm, 4 random opening plies, a 300-ply cap, `timeMs: Infinity` and 16 workers.

**How to read this.** The error bar belongs to the back rank, not to the game: `se = sd(20 rank means) / sqrt(20)`, as
`sim-configs-2026-09-13.md` §2 sets out. A row is a finding only when `|Δ| > 2 × sqrt(se_a² + se_b²)`; those rows carry a
`*`. Read every arm against its own control, never across experiments.

## 1. Re-basing: what the tuned values changed

The specs were written from the untuned values, which pinned the archer, the guard and the beast at the 1.70-pawn band floor.
The Texel fit re-priced all five fairy pieces (`sim-tuning-2026-09-13.md` §6: A 3.4, L 2.2, G < 1.5, M 2.2, S 2.7), so three
groups no longer held their armies at one value. `node tools/rebase-batch3.mjs` rewrites them. The other 17 specs stand as
`tools/mine.ts` wrote them, because each compares two arms of **one** army.

| group | was, old values | now, tuned values | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `b3-val-f0…f3` | QRRBBNN 32.0, then QRRBBNL, QRRBBML, QRRBMML, all 31.9 | QRRBBNN 30.7, QRRBNNA 31.0, QRRBNAS 30.7, QRRBASS 30.4, **new** QRRAASS 30.7 | A (3.4) and S (2.7) straddle B (3.1) and N (3.0), so one letter changes a step |
| `b3-iso-v32/27/22` | QRRBMML 31.9, QRRBAAG 27.4, RRBBAAG 21.7 | QRRBAAM 30.6, QBBNAAM 27.3, BBNNAAM 21.2 | the fairy multiset is held **exactly** at A,A,M; only the standard four step down |
| `b3-guard0/1/2` | QRRBAAN, QRRBAGN, QRRBGGN, all 28.9 | QBNNAAM 27.2, QRBNAGM 27.0, QRRBGGM 26.8 | A→G costs 1.9 pawns now, so each guard is paid for with N→R |
| `b3-paladin2/0` | QRBBNLL 29.9 against QRBBNNM 30.3 | QRRBBLL 29.1 against QRRBBMM 29.1 | L and M both price at 2.2 — the one exact swap. A knight control cannot be matched. |
| `b3-rule-*` | four toggles named | five (`+ guardCaptureLimit=1`) | so the summary passes the tuner's v0.6 test |

**Fairy count is its own effect.** Hold the army inside 0.6 pawns and four fairy pieces still cost 9.0 points of
decisiveness, add 6.6 draw points and 31 plies. Length rises monotonically and every step is flagged; draws rise in one step,
not four, because f1, f2 and f3 sit inside one error bar of each other. So the mining's raw fairy ladder (+14.5 draw points
and +43 plies from 2 to 5 fairy pieces) is about **half** material and about half the pieces. The f4 rung is new: under the
old values no army held four fairy pieces at the control's value.

**Army value does not drive pace.** Strip 9.4 pawns — a queen and a rook — from an army whose three fairy pieces never

grinds. The bottom arm also loses the queen, which `sim-mining` §2 prices at −10.8 draw points, so its draws should have
risen further than they did.

## 4. Guards (`b3-guard0/1/2`, 6 000 games)

| arm | army | score | dec | draw | capped | plies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 guards **(control)** | QBNNAAM 27.2 | 0.501 ± 0.011 | 0.727 ± 0.008 | 0.268 ± 0.008 | 0.006 ± 0.002 | 115 ± 0.9 |
| 1 guard | QRBNAGM 27.0 | 0.496 (−0.005) | 0.594 (−0.133 \*) | 0.335 (+0.067 \*) | 0.071 (+0.065 \*) | 143 (+28 \*) |
| 2 guards | QRRBGGM 26.8 | 0.515 (+0.014) | 0.370 (−0.357 \*) | 0.469 (+0.201 \*) | 0.161 (+0.155 \*) | 162 (+47 \*) |

**H10 is confirmed and then doubled: +20.1 draw points from 0 to 2 guards, not +10.** Two guards turn a 27 % draw rate into
47 % and put **16.1 %** of games on the ply cap, against a 5 % gate. Balance does not move (0.501 / 0.496 / 0.515), so the
guard is a pace knob and not a fairness knob. The buffed guard trades once and then stops: 0.65 captures a guard, **93 %
survival**, 2 rampages in 2 000 games. Take off the two N→R swaps the value match needed (`sim-configs` §4 prices a rook at
+1.6 draw points over a knight) and about +17 points belong to the guard.

## 6. Court against horde (`b3-armies`, 2 000 games, colour-reversed pairs)

Court `AAGMMQN` 24.8 pawns against horde `SSLQBBG` 24.4 pawns, tuned values. **The court scores 0.566 ± 0.019, or +46 ± 13
Elo.** The 100-game pilot read 0.790 (+225 Elo) on the old values, the old rules and a twentieth of the games. The 0.4-pawn
gap the tuned table still leaves is worth about +26 Elo at 64 Elo a pawn, so the unexplained residual is **+20 ± 13 Elo**,
inside 1.5 error bars. The tuned table fixes most of what the band floor got wrong: two beasts are no longer a two-piece
handicap.

## 7. Placement (`b3-beast-*`, `b3-archer-*`, `b3-*-G`, `b3-*-K`, 19 200 games)

| pair (arm − control), 6 400 games each | Δscore | Δdec | Δdraw | Δcapped | Δplies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| beasts a/h − beasts d/e (QRRBBSS) | +0.009 | −0.019 \* | +0.019 \* | +0.000 | +7 \* |
| archers a/h − archers d/e (QRRBBAA) | −0.011 | +0.006 | −0.005 | −0.001 | +6 \* |
| archer beside a guard − archer away (QRBAAGG) | +0.006 | +0.012 | −0.009 | −0.003 | −1 |
| maester beside the king − maesters ≥ 3 files off (QRRBBMM) | **+0.021 \*** | −0.017 | +0.020 | −0.003 | +0 |

**The corner no longer kills a short-range piece.** The mining measured a corner beast idle in 31 % of games and a corner
archer shooting 5.4× less than a centre archer. Under v0.6 the corner costs 6 to 7 plies and nothing else: a cornered beast
makes 5.3 moves a piece against 5.0 in the centre, a cornered archer fires 3.70 shots a game against 4.11. `archerMove=any`
and `beastMove=any` paid that debt already. **H4 fails**: the guard does not wall the archer in. **A maester next to the king
is worth +0.021 to White (+15 Elo)** — the largest placement effect the project has measured and the first to clear the bar
on balance. The swap is a free developing move and White plays it first.

## 8. Rule removals, on today's rules (`b3-rule-*`, 8 000 games, one shared rank set)

| arm | score | dec | draw | capped | plies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base = v0.6 **(control)** | 0.533 ± 0.008 | 0.605 ± 0.011 | 0.317 ± 0.010 | 0.078 ± 0.005 | 141 ± 2.1 |
| `archerChecks=false` | 0.531 (−0.002) | 0.581 (−0.024) | 0.343 (+0.026) | 0.076 (−0.002) | 145 (+3) |
| `guardImmune=false` | 0.541 (+0.008) | 0.656 (+0.051 \*) | 0.297 (−0.020) | 0.046 (−0.032 \*) | 128 (−14 \*) |
| `maesterLongSwap=false` | 0.536 (+0.003) | 0.603 (−0.002) | 0.310 (−0.007) | 0.086 (+0.008) | 140 (−2) |

**A takeable guard is the cheapest cure for the draw.** Guard survival falls 92 % → 66 %, guard captures 1.18 → 0.45 a game,
decisiveness rises 5.1 points and the capped share falls **7.8 % → 4.6 %, under the gate**, at no cost to balance. **The
archer can give up the check**: nothing flags, and checks fall 7.85 → 6.91 a game. **The long swap costs nothing**: Δscore
+0.003 ± 0.012 is +2 ± 8 Elo and the bar excludes H9's 15–40 Elo. The event counter calls every maester-king swap "long", so
the rule removes only the distant ones, 1.18 → 0.87 swaps a game.

## 9. Mirroring, the beast, and the paladin

**Mirroring hides little (`b3-mirror-*`, 4 000 games).** With one rank on both sides the spread across 20 ranks is 0.035
against a sampling floor of 0.037 — no signal, 95 % bound 19 Elo. Draw each side its own rank from one army and the spread is
0.052 against a floor of 0.038: a true spread of 0.036 at **1.7σ**, which does not flag. Two arrangements of one army differ
by under 40 Elo (95 %). Under-powered, not negative: 100 games a pairing cannot resolve 25 Elo.

**`b3-nobeast` has no beast to remove.** `tools/mine.ts` gave it `b3-rule-base`'s army (`QLRBAGM`), so the pair is a
replication and not a test — every metric lands inside the bar (Δscore +0.004, Δdraw −0.002, Δplies +3), which makes a useful
noise floor. The artefact it meant to test is dead anyway: the buffed beast makes 5.0 moves a piece and `minFairyUse` reads
1.57 on the two-beast ranks, over the term's cap of 1. The guard and the paladin now set that gate.

**Two paladins against two maesters at the same 29.1 pawns (`b3-paladin*`, 4 000 games).** White scores 0.581 ± 0.008 with
the paladins and 0.494 ± 0.008 with the maesters: **Δ +0.087, or +61 Elo of first-move advantage**, with decisiveness +0.200,
draws −0.197 and 26 plies less. This is White's edge on each rank, not L against M head to head, so it does not re-price the
paladin. It does say that two pieces the tuned table calls equal make two different games, and that a paladin-rich rank is
six times more lopsided than the 9-Elo spread the sweep reported.

## 10. Does the interest order survive? (`b3-interest-d3/d4`, 4 800 games)

The 20 highest and 20 lowest ranks by residualised interest in `sweep-p2`, replayed on today's rules and the tuned
evaluation, 60 games a rank, the same openings at both depths. Interest is residualised on draw rate inside these 40 ranks in
every column, so the four share one definition. `sweep-buff.r1` holds the same 40 ranks under the buff set and the untuned
evaluation, which separates the rules from the fit.

| Spearman ρ over 40 ranks | residual interest | raw interest | draw rate | score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **depth 3 → depth 4** (today) | **+0.81** | +0.70 | +0.59 | +0.24 |
| tuned depth 3 → untuned depth 3 (`sweep-p2`) | **−0.16** | −0.10 | +0.66 | +0.02 |
| tuned depth 3 → untuned buffed depth 3 (`sweep-buff`) | +0.37 | +0.74 | +0.68 | +0.17 |
| untuned `sweep-p2` → untuned `sweep-buff` | +0.03 | −0.03 | +0.49 | −0.03 |

**The order survives depth and does not survive the rules.** One more ply keeps it almost whole (ρ = +0.81 residualised,
+0.70 raw). The pre-buff ranking carries no information about today's game: ρ = −0.16, and the two groups it separated by
**+0.093 ± 0.015** in `sweep-p2` are separated by **−0.003 ± 0.007** at depth 3 and **−0.003 ± 0.005** at depth 4
(`sweep-buff` already reads −0.015 ± 0.018). The reason is the one `sim-mining` §9 named: the 20 "most interesting" ranks
hold **2 beasts between them and the 20 "least interesting" hold 25**, and `beastMove=any` removed the beast's utilisation
deficit, so the selection now sorts nothing. Interest itself is a stable measurement (ρ = +0.74 raw between an untuned engine
and today's) and draw rate is stable throughout (+0.49 to +0.68). **Do not reuse any interest ranking taken before v0.6.**

## 11. What this suggests

**Pool rules.** The guard is the draw engine and nothing else is close: two cost 20 draw points and put 16 % of games on the
cap. Two levers fix it, both measured here — `guardImmune=false` (7.8 % → 4.6 % capped, +5.1 decisiveness) or a cap of one
guard a rank. Fairy density still costs pace at a fixed value, so a pool that makes fairy pieces easy to draw stays slow
however it is priced.

**Army design.** Stop pricing armies by weight. Nine pawns of standard material changed nothing measurable; two guards
changed everything. The tuned values are close to right in bulk — the court/horde gap fell from +225 Elo to an unexplained
+20 ± 13 — but L and M share a price and do not share a game, so the table prices material and not tempo.

**Placement.** Almost nothing again, with one exception: a maester beside the king is worth +15 Elo to White. The 2017 corner
penalty is gone. Add no placement rule, but keep the maester off the king's neighbour square if the setup ever needs a
tie-break.

**Rule combinations.** The v0.6 set is sound. The archer's check and the maester's long swap are both free to remove, so both
stay available as trims. `guardImmune` is the one toggle here that buys decisiveness, and it buys it cheaply.

**32 runs, 76 100 games, 8 168 s of engine time (2 h 16 m), 9.3 games/s over the batch.** Depth 3 ran at 8 to 21 games/s, by
how long the armies play; depth 4 ran at 1.45. Wall clock, tests and analysis included: 2 h 26 m. Nothing was cut.

**What this does not show.** Every headline is depth 3 on 20 placements an arm; only the interest order was replayed deeper.
The guard and paladin arms use mirrored ranks, so they measure White's edge and pace and not one piece against another — the
values pass still needs its own asymmetric arms. `sweep-p2` ran the pre-buff rules **and** the untuned evaluation, so §10's
`p2 → d3` row moves two things at once; the `sweep-buff` row separates them.
