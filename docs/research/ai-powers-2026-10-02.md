# A stronger computer player for the kings' powers mode — 2026-10-02

Goal (owner request via the main session, 2026-10-02): make the computer player stronger, above all
in the new kings' powers mode. Work on branch `ai-powers`; nothing deployed.

## Summary

**The computer player is stronger, and the new net is adopted** (`src/ai/nnue/weights.ts`). Against
the net it replaces, in colour-swapped pairs:

| games | depth 3 (800 games each) | 800 ms a move, the browser's Club setting (120 games each) |
|---|---|---|
| (a) ordinary games, no powers | **+89 ± 22 Elo** (score 62.5%) | **+80 ± 64 Elo** (61.3%) |
| (b) kings' powers games | **+68 ± 21 Elo** (59.6%) | **+114 ± 48 Elo** (65.8%) |

Every range is above zero. Two things made the difference: the net now **sees the kings' powers**, and
it now **sees the Ogre**: until today the game switched the net off whenever an Ogre was on the board,
which happens in about half of all random armies. Beginner and Casual are still weak (each lost all 20
of its games against Club).

## What was wrong with the old evaluation

The browser's computer player judges positions with the hand-written evaluation plus a small
learned correction (the "residual net", adopted 2026-09-17). Two gaps:

1. **It knew nothing about the kings' powers.** The net was trained before the powers existed, so it
   saw only the pieces. A king that can freeze a piece, or pieces sheltered by Mercy or Holy Light,
   looked exactly like an ordinary position to it.
2. **It switched itself off whenever an Ogre was on the board.** The Ogre joined the random-army pool
   on 2026-09-25, after the net was trained. The net has no inputs for it, so on any board with an Ogre the game
   quietly fell back to the hand-written evaluation alone. About **47% of random and daily armies**
   include an Ogre (7 picks from a pool of 15 that holds one), so in roughly half of all games the
   net was not used at all.

A third, smaller point: the piece rules changed after the net was trained (the archer's shots, the
beast's forward capture, promotion choices, the Paladin left the pool), so its judgement was a
little out of date even in ordinary games.

## What was built

- **Two new input blocks in the net** (`src/ai/nnue/net.ts`): the Ogre on each square (for each side),
  and each side's king power while it can still act (an always-on power, or one with uses left; a
  spent one-use power is no longer shown). The download grows from 121 kB to 134 kB of text for the
  net. A net written before today still loads and plays exactly as before (checked: identical moves,
  scores and node counts on three positions, and unit tests).
- **A data, training and match tool** (`src/sim/powers-net.ts`): plays games under the official
  powers rules (`POWERS_BALANCED`, as the game applies them), keeps the quiet positions with the
  search's score, fine-tunes the adopted net, and plays colour-swapped matches.
- **Skill-level check** in the same tool: Beginner and Casual against Club, using the game's own skill
  settings.

## Data and training

- **Corpus:** 2,280 games at depth 3, played by the current browser engine, in a 2-hour slot on 4
  cores (planned 6,000; scaled down to fit the compute budget). Each game drew its kings: about a
  third with no powers (727 games, 31.9%), the rest giving each side one of the twelve powers or none.
  48% of the armies held an Ogre. **176,452 positions** kept (17,532 held out for checking), 118,292
  of them from games with powers. For comparison, the adopted net learned from 7.8 million positions,
  so this run **fine-tunes** that net instead of training a new one.
- **Training:** four variants, all a few minutes on one core. Held-out error (lower is better):

| net | ordinary positions | positions with a power | boards with an Ogre | all |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| adopted net (start) | 0.001442 | 0.001223 | 0.001172 | 0.001314 |
| **fine-tuned, chosen (`c-lr1`)** | **0.001333** | **0.001170** | **0.001163** | **0.001238** |
| fine-tuned, slower learning rate | 0.001334 | 0.001173 | 0.001168 | 0.001240 |
| fine-tuned, faster learning rate | 0.001329 | 0.001174 | 0.001172 | 0.001239 |
| only the new inputs trained | 0.001436 | 0.001186 | 0.001154 | 0.001290 |

  All fine-tuned versions were best after one or two passes over the data; more passes made them
  worse on the held-out positions, so more data is the obvious next step. ("Boards with an Ogre" for
  the starting net is the net blind to the Ogre; in play that net never ran on those boards.)

## Matches: new net against the adopted one

Colour-swapped pairs: both games of a pair use the same army, the same random opening and the same two
kings; the kings stay with their colour and the two engines change sides, so first-move and power
advantages cancel within the pair. Random armies from the game's own pool (so about half contain an
Ogre). Elo is the candidate's advantage with its 95% range.

| match | games | candidate score | Elo ± 95% | draws | mean length |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) no powers, depth 3 | 800 (400 pairs) | 0.625 | **+88.7 ± 22.2** | 12.9% | 99 plies |
| (b) powers, depth 3 | 800 (400 pairs) | 0.596 | **+67.7 ± 21.2** | 9.3% | 93 plies |
| (a) no powers, 800 ms a move | 120 (60 pairs) | 0.613 | **+79.5 ± 64.4** | 10.8% | 109 plies |
| (b) powers, 800 ms a move | 120 (60 pairs) | 0.658 | **+113.9 ± 47.5** | 15.0% | 103 plies |

In (b) both sides were drawn at random from the twelve powers and "no power", never both "no power"
(353 of the 400 depth-3 pairs had a power on both sides). The 800 ms matches are the browser's own
setting (Club, the default) and include the speed cost of the new net; they are small (scaled down
to fit the compute budget), so their ranges are wide, but they agree with the depth-3 results.

Where the gain comes from (depth 3, split by army; pair-based ranges):

| | army with an Ogre | army without an Ogre |
|---|---|---|
| (a) no powers | +159 ± 36 (197 pairs) | +27 ± 32 (203 pairs) |
| (b) powers | +109 ± 31 (192 pairs) | +31 ± 31 (208 pairs) |

So the largest gain is the Ogre; on boards without one the new net is a little better too, but that
part alone is within noise at this sample size. At 800 ms in powers games the gain without an Ogre was
+104 ± 65 (31 pairs).

## Decision

**Adopted.** The bar set for this work: not worse in ordinary games (within noise) and better in powers
games, as the 2026-09-16 acceptance did it — a large fixed-depth decision match whose 95% range must
clear zero, then a confirmation at a closer-to-browser setting that must not change sign. Both
modes clear zero at depth 3, and both confirmations at 800 ms a move are positive and also clear zero.
The new blob is in `src/ai/nnue/weights.ts`; the old one is kept, pinned, in
`sim/nnue-powers/current.arm.json` (do not re-run `train` before copying it elsewhere: `train`
rewrites that file from whatever `weights.ts` holds).

## Other checks

- **Speed** (`powers-net.ts bench`, 16 positions, half with powers, one core): the new net searches
  50k positions a second against 55k for the old one (about 9% slower, because it now also runs on Ogre
  boards), and a one-second search reaches depth 5.00 against 4.81. The 800 ms matches already include
  this cost.
- **Skill levels** (the game's own settings, 800 ms thinking time, 10 pairs per level, half with powers):
  Beginner 0 of 20 against Club, Casual 0 of 20 against Club. Both stay clearly weaker.
- **The browser's AI worker** (`src/ai/worker.ts`) loads the net through `setEvaluator('residual')`;
  with the new blob that succeeds, it reports Ogre inputs, and the built worker bundle contains the new
  net and not the old one.
- **Old nets still load**: a net written before today loads with the new rows at zero and plays
  exactly as before (identical move, score and node count on three test positions against the base
  commit; unit tests in `src/ai/nnue/net.test.ts`).
- `npx tsc --noEmit` clean; `npx vitest run --exclude '**/sim.test.ts'` 305 of 305 pass; `npm run build`
  passes.
- **Compute used:** about 5 hours of the 4 cores (16:07–21:05 UTC): corpus 2 h, matches 2 h 35 min,
  training, bench, skill check, tests and build the rest; plus a few minutes of small smoke tests while
  the balance tournament was still running.

## Not done

- **Policy / move ordering** (`docs/research/policy-distillation-plan-2026-09-17.md`): not started.
  The compute budget (about 6 hours on 4 cores) went to the corpus and the matches; a policy change is
  only adopted after a match at equal thinking time, which there was no time left to play.
- Only 2,280 games of data. The net stopped improving after one or two passes, so a larger corpus
  (and later training from scratch) should gain more, especially for the powers.

## Reproduce

```
tsx src/sim/powers-net.ts gen --id pn-g1 --games 6000 --depth 3 --workers 4 --seed 20261002   # stopped at 2,280 games
tsx src/sim/powers-net.ts train --data pn-g1 --name c-lr1 --lr 0.001 --batch 1024 --epochs 12
tsx src/sim/powers-net.ts match --id pm-none-d3 --cand sim/nnue-powers/c-lr1.arm.json --pairs 400 --depth 3 --mode none --seed 7201
tsx src/sim/powers-net.ts match --id pm-pow-d3  --cand sim/nnue-powers/c-lr1.arm.json --pairs 400 --depth 3 --mode powers --seed 7202
tsx src/sim/powers-net.ts match --id pm-none-t800 --cand sim/nnue-powers/c-lr1.arm.json --pairs 60 --timeMs 800 --mode none --seed 7301
tsx src/sim/powers-net.ts match --id pm-pow-t800  --cand sim/nnue-powers/c-lr1.arm.json --pairs 60 --timeMs 800 --mode powers --seed 7302
```

Records: `sim/out/pn-g1.jsonl` and `sim/out/pm-*.jsonl` (game lists, not in Git), `sim/out/pm-*.result.json`,
`sim/nnue-powers/` (training logs, `c-lr1.train.json` with the corpus and model fingerprints, the
match arms, the candidate weights; the 12.7 MB corpus `pn-g1.bin` is not in Git).
