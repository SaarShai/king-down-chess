# A small NNUE for King Down, and the guard refit under it (2026-09-13)

Batch-2 run **R6**. Two pieces of work, one measured and shipped, one measured and **not** shipped.

| stage | what | result |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Texel refit after the guard rule changed | **+94 ± 30 Elo at depth 3. Shipped.** |
| 1 | `1408 -> 32x2 -> 1` perspective net, Int16, in the search | **−129 ± 29 (d3), −98 ± 40 (d4). Not shipped.** |
| 2 | second self-play round | out of time: the shared machine gave 1.3 games/s |

The net works — deterministic, 1.19x slower than the hand evaluation, and a **better** predictor
of the engine's own search score than the hand evaluation is. It still loses, and §5 says why. It
sits in the tree behind `setEvaluator('nnue')`; the default stays linear.

## 1. Stage 0 — the guard value was fitted for a different guard

`GUARD_V = 124` was fitted on games where a guard could take a pawn, slide two squares and spend
its one capture. The shipped rules give the guard its 2017 identity back: immortal, one step,
**never captures** (`docs/RULES.md` §6.9). Every number fitted beside it met the wrong piece.

**Data.** `tune-g0`: 8,693 games, depth 3, 1,000 random back ranks, seed 101, all five v0.6 toggles
named on the command line so the run describes itself; White scored 0.5168 over 135.2 plies. Two
other campaigns shared the machine, so it held 3–9 games/s against the first pass's 11.8 and
stopped at 8,693 of a planned 20,000. 6,222 of those games → **128,516 quiet positions** (12,865
held out; 6,969 in check and 14,122 noisy dropped), through the unchanged `src/sim/tune.ts` path.
`K` refitted to **0.814** (it was 0.667): under the new guard the same evaluation is a *steeper*
predictor of the result.

| fit | train MSE | validation MSE |
|---|---|---|
| shipped values | 0.121931 | 0.121672 |
| **λ = 1e-4** | **0.120126** | **0.119517** |
| λ = 1e-3 | 0.121067 | 0.120269 |

**Match**, λ = 1e-4 against the shipped evaluation, colour-swapped pairs, random back ranks, table
cleared every ply on both sides: 400 games, 200 pairs, pentanomial [19, 13, 72, 35, 61], score
0.632, **+94 ± 30 Elo**, nElo +104, LOS 100.0%, 9.8% draws, 145 plies. Applied.

| | P | N | B | R | Q | A | L | **G** | M | S |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| was | 100 | 296 | 313 | 472 | 909 | 295 | 318 | **124** | 291 | 261 |
| now | 100 | 316 | 322 | 449 | 933 | 337 | 326 | **96** | 320 | 308 |

**The guard falls 124 → 96**, the one value the rule change points at, and the only one that falls
by more than the rook's 23. Everything else rises 3–18%, which is the K change read from the other
end: with the pawn anchored at 100 and a steeper K, a piece buys more pawns than it did. Weights:
tempo 5 → **0**, mobility (B/R/Q/L) 1/1/0/0 → **0/2/−1/1**, beast target 8 → 10 (cap 18), shield
open 4 → 3, maester within 1 of its king 11 → 8, `PHASE_MAX` 5712 → 5681.

A tempo of 0 and a *negative* queen mobility weight are the fit saying those terms are noise at
this data size; the first pass already cut the hand-set mobility weights fourfold, and this one
pushes two of them through zero.

## 2. Stage 1 — the data

`src/sim/gen.ts sample` replays each game through its own notation and keeps a position when the
ply has a search score inside ±2000 cp, the mover is not in check, and `evaluate()` agrees with a
full quiescence search within 50 cp — the Texel sampler's "quiet". **Every** ply is a candidate,
not 30 a game: labels are per position, so thinning buys nothing.

7,622 games → **863,295 positions**, 86,075 held out by *game*. Dropped: 30,488 unscored (the four
random opening plies), 1,667 decided, 49,741 in check, 84,807 noisy. 114 positions a game, 68 bytes
each, 56 MB. Each row carries the board, the side to move, the game result and **the search score
the engine already recorded when it chose that move** (`PlyRecord.cp`) — so a labelled corpus costs
one simulation run and no second search.

Two things this data is not: **863k positions, not the 5–10M** `ai-players.md` §2 asks for, and
labelled by the **pre-stage-0** evaluation — so the net distils an engine 94 Elo weaker than the
one it must beat.

## 3. Stage 1 — the net

`1408 -> 32x2 -> 1`. Inputs are 2 relations (mine / theirs) × 11 piece types × 64 squares seen from
the side to move; Black's view is the board mirrored (`s ^ 56`), so one net plays both colours and
**the evaluation is exactly colour-symmetric by construction** (`net.test.ts` checks 200 boards).
No king buckets, so nothing forces a refresh. Both accumulators are clipped to [0, 1], concatenated
`[mine, theirs]` and read by one output row.

**Quantisation.** `w1_int = round(w1 * QA)`, `w2_int = round(w2 * QB)`, **QA = 16384, QB = 4096**,
SCALE = 400, accumulator in Int32. Not the hobby `QA=255, QB=64` [CPW NNUE]: those are sized for an
int8 output layer, and at that precision the round-off of *this* net is about 10 cp — the error is
`sqrt(pieces)/QA × sqrt(2·HIDDEN) × |w2| × SCALE`, and nothing here is wide enough to average it
away. The price is headroom: an Int16 weight may not pass `32767/QA = 2.0`, which the trainer
clamps to during training and not after. Measured against the trainer's own float forward pass on
1,000 random boards: **max 0.713 cp, mean 0.252 cp** (0.942 / 0.280 on an inflated random net).
45,153 weights, one base64 line, **117.6 kB**, bundled in the source.

**The trainer is TypeScript, not PyTorch.** It imports `featureIndex` from the inference module, so
the forward pass it fits and the one the engine runs cannot disagree about what an input means —
the failure mode a separate trainer exists to create. 12 s an epoch on 777k positions, one thread.

## 4. Stage 1 — training

The first net used the recipe the research prescribes, `0.5 × result + 0.5 × sigmoid(score)`. It
overfitted from the second epoch and never came back:

| net | target | epochs | best val | at epoch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0.5 result + 0.5 sigmoid(score) | 30 | 0.034053 | **1** |
| — | 0.2 result + 0.8 sigmoid(score) | 5 | 0.007141 | **2** |
| **B** | **sigmoid(score) only** | 16 | 0.001593 | **16** |
| C | score in centipawns, plain MSE | 14 | 0.024331 (62.4 cp RMS) | 12 |

**The game result is the memorisable half of the target, and King Down makes that worse than chess
does.** Every game starts from its own random back rank out of 1,373 piece sets, one result covers
all 114 of its positions, and 45k feature weights easily learn "this army beat that army": the WDL
term's real sample size is 6,860 *games*, not 777k positions. Dropping it turns the net into a
distillation of the engine's own depth-3 search, and held-out error then falls for all 16 epochs.
Net B is what `weights.ts` holds.

## 5. Stage 1 — the measurement, and why it lost

nnue against linear, one process, evaluations swapped between plies with the transposition table
dropped, colour-swapped pairs, random back ranks. Both sides keep the same material values, so move
ordering and delta pruning are identical and only the leaf evaluation differs.

| match | games | pairs | pentanomial | nnue score | Elo ± 95% | nElo | draws | plies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B, depth 3 | 400 | 200 | [75, 27, 74, 13, 11] | 0.323 | **−129 ± 29** | −147 | 5.5% | 137 |
| C, depth 3 | 400 | 200 | [92, 31, 66, 4, 7] | 0.254 | **−187 ± 26** | −222 | 7.2% | 132 |
| B, depth 4 | 200 | 100 | [27, 23, 35, 8, 7] | 0.362 | **−98 ± 40** | −116 | 13.0% | 142 |

The gap narrows from 129 to 98 Elo with one more ply, which is the shape of an evaluation deficit
a deeper search partly repairs — not of a bug, which a ply would not touch.

**Speed is not the reason.** In Node, on 12 positions, on an idle moment of the same machine:
linear 194k nodes/s, nnue 163k nodes/s — **1.19x**, under the 2x that would have forced an
incremental accumulator, and at *fixed depth* a slower evaluation costs no strength at all. A
one-second search reaches depth **6.0** linear and **5.8** with the net, so the browser gate
(depth ≥ 5 in one second) passes either way; under full load both fall to 4.7.

**Accuracy is not the reason either**, and this is the interesting part. On 40,000 held-out
positions, root-mean-square error against the depth-3 search score:

| | linear (shipped) | net B | net C |
|---|---|---|---|
| RMS vs the search score | 74.9 cp | **63.9 cp** | **60.9 cp** |
| reads a missing queen as | 931 cp | 549 cp | 625 cp |

Both nets predict the thing they were trained on better than the evaluation that beats them, and
the net with the *best* prediction plays the *worst*. This is `sim-tuning-2026-09-13.md` §3 again,
one level up: **held-out error ranks fits, a match decides them.**

The queen row is the mechanism. A net fitted on quiet self-play positions meets few large material
imbalances and regresses them to the middle, pricing a whole queen at 60%. Inside alpha-beta that
is fatal: every capture is under-rewarded and the engine trades into losses a linear material term
would refuse. Fitting centipawns instead of sigmoids recovers part of the scale (549 → 625) and
**plays 58 Elo worse**, because it spends the same 45k parameters on the ±2000 cp tail instead of
the ±100 cp band where moves are chosen.

## 6. What shipped, and what is in the tree

Shipped: **stage 0 only** — `src/ai/eval.ts`, refitted, +94 ± 30 Elo, guard 124 → 96. In the tree,
off by default: `src/ai/nnue/net.ts` (Int16 inference, full refresh per leaf),
`weights.ts` (net B), `net.test.ts` (quantisation, symmetry, determinism, blob round-trip),
`src/sim/gen.ts` (`sample` / `train` / `pack` / `bench` / `arms`), and `setEvaluator()` in
`src/ai/eval.ts`. The search calls `evalBoard`; `evaluate()` and `evaluateBoard()` stay linear for
the tuner and the balance lab, which price pieces through them.

A match between two evaluators needed no change to `src/sim/*`: `EvalParams` carries an optional
`evaluator` field, and `src/sim/game.ts` already swaps an `EvalParams` per side between plies and
drops the transposition table with it. The blob is bundled twice — page and worker both import the
search — so the browser build grows 643 → 765 kB and the worker 16 → 137 kB, 902 kB in all.

## 7. Next, in the order that pays

1. **Ten times the data, from the shipped engine.** 863k positions from a 94-Elo-weaker teacher is
   the whole explanation on offer; the research asks for 5–10M, and one JS net reached parity with
   a mature hand evaluation at about 8M. A night of idle machine, not new code.
2. **Keep a material term outside the net.** Evaluate as `material + net(board)` and fit the net to
   the residual. It removes exactly the failure §5 measures, costs one addition per leaf, and keeps
   the queen at 933 cp by construction. Untested, and the obvious first experiment.
3. **Hold the result term out until the data is large.** Restore the WDL blend only when there are
   more games than feature weights; below that it is a memorisation channel.
4. **Then, and only then, incremental accumulators.** `write()` in `src/ai/search.ts` already knows
   the old and new piece on every square it touches, so the update is fifteen lines. It buys nodes
   per second and no strength at fixed depth, which is how every match here is scored.

```
npm run nnue -- train --epochs 16 --batch 8192 --lr 0.015 --loss wdl --lambda 0
npm run nnue -- arms && npm run nnue -- bench --depth 5 --positions 12
npm run tune -- match --id nnue-d3 --games 400 --depth 3 --seed 6102 \
  --tuned sim/nnue/eval-nnue.json --base sim/nnue/eval-linear.json    # −129 ± 29
npx tsc --noEmit && npx vitest run && npx vite build
```

## 9. What this does not show

- **One data distribution, one shape, one width.** N = 32 came from the 200 kB blob budget
  (N = 64 is 235 kB), not from an experiment.
- **Stage 0 re-priced every piece on 128k positions**, a fifth of the first pass. The guard's
  direction is safe; the 3–18% rise everywhere else is one match wide.
- **Net C was matched at depth 3 only**: the gate failed by seven error bars there.
- **Speed is a Node proxy for the browser.** Same code, no worker hop, no rendering.
