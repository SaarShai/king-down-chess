## Rating systems for one-against-one games [`rk-ratings`]

Most online one-against-one game sites use the Glicko family. Lichess, PyChess and OGS use Glicko-2. Chess.com uses Glicko. Pokémon Showdown shows Glicko-1 next to Elo. FIDE, US Chess and Board Game Arena use Elo variants. Xbox uses TrueSkill, but Microsoft lets only Xbox games and non-commercial projects use it. OpenSkill (MIT) is the open alternative, and it is best for teams.

Lichess is the most complete public model for King Down:
- Glicko-2 updates after each game.
- New players start at 1500 with a wide deviation.
- A "?" shows while the deviation is 110 or more.
- The deviation grows with time off; points do not decay.
- The floor is 400.
- Computer games are unrated.
- Only signed-in players play rated games.
- Checks stop rating farming.

Since November 2025, Lichess also adds a first-move term to Glicko-2, about 12 Elo in standard chess. Few other products adjust for the first move. King Down's engine tests put White near 0.53 (about +24 Elo). So King Down needs balanced colours, the same term, or both. Today the ChatGPT invite flow always seats the friend as Black.

For a small pool:
- Keep few rating categories: one for standard King Down and one for card mode.
- Match widely.
- Use lower leaderboard gates.
- Watch for rating drift.

Store rating, deviation, volatility, game count and last-rated time in Supabase. Only the match authority writes them, in the same transaction as the result, for both surfaces. For code, port the MIT scalachess Glicko calculator, which has the colour and time terms, or use glicko2-lite (MIT). Do not copy GPL or AGPL code, and do not use TrueSkill. Note: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. Some details, such as the Chess.com rating floor, are therefore not confirmed.

## Ranks, leagues, seasons and leaderboards [`rk-ladders`]

I studied 11 products and the published research. The products are Chess.com, Lichess, Duolingo, Hearthstone, League of Legends, Valorant, Marvel Snap, Clash Royale, Board Game Arena, Steam and Google Play Games. Five patterns repeat across them.

1. A hidden skill rating sets the matches. A separate visible rank or points ladder gives the feeling of progress.
2. Most products run cycles: weekly cohorts, monthly or quarterly seasons, or yearly placements. A new cycle resets only the visible rank, never the hidden rating.
3. Most products protect players from losses. They use floors, no demotion in low tiers, and badges for the best rank in the season.
4. Leaderboards admit only players who have enough games and played recently. Many platforms also show friends and "around me" views.
5. All the systems I checked accept results only from the server and penalize sandbagging and collusion.

The research supports leaderboards with conditions. A leaderboard acts like a difficult goal. It helps players near the top. Results for players at the bottom are mixed or bad. A loss streak almost doubles the 7-day churn risk in one EA game. Students prefer "around me" boards but also want to see the top 5.

The data for small player bases is clear. Chess.com waits until a division has 50 players. Board Game Arena changes its number of leagues with the popularity of each game. Duolingo builds cohorts from players with similar activity and time zone. The first two rules show that a fixed cohort size fails when few people play.

My recommendation for King Down: one shared Glicko-2 rating, named bands with floors, and short top lists with an "around me" row. Add weekly leagues later, with cohorts that change size, and add quarterly seasons. Show a compact board in ChatGPT. My web search budget ran out before the end, so I could not check some facts. ## Profiles, achievements, badges, awards and progression [`rk-profiles`]

The products I checked use two progress systems and keep them apart. A skill rating (Glicko or Elo) goes up and down, and new players see a provisional mark. A progression track (XP, collection level, crowns) only goes up. Chess.com, Lichess and OGS show a rating for each time control or variant, with game counts and history. BGA, Hearthstone, Marvel Snap and Duolingo add XP, levels and achievements on top. Xbox, Steam and PlayStation give clear achievement rules: 10 to 100 achievements, one early unlock, about half reachable in normal play, progress bars, few hidden items and rarity percentages. For asynchronous play, all four turn-based sites I checked track reliability. They use a timeout percentage (Chess.com), karma and finish rate (BGA), vacation days (Chess.com, OGS) or play bans that are not public (Lichess). Self-determination research supports badges as feedback on competence. But rewards that players expect and that depend on results can lower intrinsic motivation, and gamification effects fade over long periods. Duolingo data shows that slack (freezes, grace days) helps streaks. Lists of dark patterns flag daily rewards, playing by appointment, fear of missing out (FOMO) and complete-the-collection. ChatGPT Apps rules allow at most two actions on an inline card and do not allow a plugin to sell digital goods. For King Down: keep one rating for each rated queue, on a fixed piece pool. Keep crowns as a learning track that you never lose. Add a small achievement set and show a finish rate on profiles. Do not use daily-login pressure. The shared web-search budget ran out during the research, so a few product facts are not verified.

## Fair play, cheating and rating abuse [`rk-integrity`]

Large chess sites catch engine use in two steps. First, cheap signals choose which games to examine. Then engine-match statistics and human review decide. Lichess sends a game to engine analysis after an upset, very even move times, many tab switches (blurs), or a win by a new player. Irwin and Kaladin then score the player, and moderators make the decision. Chess.com closes 105k to 164k accounts each month, and automatic rules make about 85% of these closures. New accounts are the main risk. Both sites give rating points back to victims. Both sites treat abort, leave and stall as a separate problem. They use first-move timers, a "claim victory" button and timeouts that grow with each repeat. Sandbagging and boosting are found with simple counts of short games and of wins against the same opponent.

For King Down, the real risk is the site itself. The web app loads any position with ?fen= and runs the only King Down engine in the browser. In ChatGPT, the model can see the board in structuredContent. The server already refuses the computer tool in friend games. Correspondence games cannot be policed anywhere.

Do these first: (1) rated play only for signed-in accounts, with provisional ratings and a minimum-games gate on the leaderboard; (2) abort, leave and stall rules with automatic timeouts; (3) keep per-move times and focus-loss flags; (4) turn off the local engine while a rated game is live, as Pychess does; (5) a report button and a manual review queue; (6) automatic rating refunds. Note: the shared web-search budget ran out early, so most evidence comes from primary pages and source code fetched directly.

**Product notes**

- **Lichess**: This is the deepest primary source, because the lila server is open source. Irwin (engine analysis plus a neural network) and Kaladin (a CNN on insights data) send cases to about 100 volunteer moderators. Cheap triage chooses games for analysis: upsets, flat move times, blurs, and wins by new players. SandbagWatch counts short rated games. Playbans grow with repeats for abort, leave and sit. The opponent can claim victory after a scaled timeout. Accounts are linked through shared IPs and fingerprints. Marked accounts are paired only with each other. Victims get refunds of up to 150 points if they were not provisional. Leaderboards need 30 games, recent activity and a low RD. Correspondence games and friend games are outside automatic assessment. <https://github.com/lichess-org/lila> <https://lichess.org/faq> <https://lichess.org/terms-of-service> <https://lichess.org/page/fair-play>
- **Chess.com**: Fair play at very large scale: about 20M games a day, more than 100 factors, about 85% automatic closures, and a team of more than 30 people with a titled panel. Closures each month are 105k to 164k (2025-2026), and about 40% of closed accounts are no more than 2 weeks old. Rating refunds are automatic and usually uncapped, and the cheater's ongoing games go to the opponents. A second account needs approval and may never play the main one. Abort limits and a 5-minute wait apply to bad sportsmanship. Daily chess permits the Opening Explorer and books. Chess.com proposed (July 2025) that new accounts play new accounts. I could not read the disconnect and abandon rules because the search budget ran out. <https://www.chess.com/cheating> <https://www.chess.com/legal/fair-play> <https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8588324-what-happens-when-someone-is-caught-cheating> <https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8568381-can-i-have-multiple-accounts> <https://www.chess.com/blog/FairPlay/breaking-down-100-000-closures-a-month>
- **OGS (Online Go Server)**: Every game gets a tool pass, and a small AI-detection team works with community moderators. Three CMs must agree before an action. Cheated games are annulled, plus enough other games to repair ratings. Escaping and stalling have their own guidelines and a warning ladder, and the app has appeal help flows. Escaping is allowed in correspondence games, and this ruling is under review. Much of the moderation code is public on GitHub. <https://github.com/online-go/online-go.com/wiki/Moderation-at-OGS> <https://github.com/online-go/online-go.com/wiki/CM-Guideline---Stalling> <https://github.com/online-go/online-go.com/wiki/CM-Guidelines---Stopped-Playing-(aka-Escaping)>

Any opponent can skip the turn of a player whose clock is negative, or expel that player. Turn-based mode gives days per game. The only readable sources are doc pages from 2018 and 2020. The current FAQ loads with JavaScript and I could not read it, so details such as reputation filters on tables need a check. <https://en.doc.boardgamearena.com/Game_clock> <https://en.doc.boardgamearena.com/Faq>
