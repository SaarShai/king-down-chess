A completeness critic read the three sections and named six gaps. One research agent studied each gap. Then an independent checker read every finding against primary sources (2026-10-10). The sections cite these ids in square brackets.

## Gap 1 (cross): In October 2026, which ChatGPT users can install and use a third-party plugin (app) that has an MCP Apps UI and OAuth? Check plans (Free, Go, Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, Edu), regions (EU/EEA, UK), teen accounts (13-17) and clients (web, desktop, iOS, Android). Also: how does a second person add a private or unlisted plugin that is not yet in the directory (developer mode only, workspace sharing, or a share link)? [`gap1`]

Short answer (2026-10-10): a friend can open a private King Down plugin only if they add it to their own ChatGPT by hand, as a "custom MCP server". This works only on ChatGPT on the web. The friend opens chatgpt.com/plugins, selects +, then "Add custom MCP server". They paste the server URL, accept an "elevated risk" warning and sign in through OAuth.

OpenAI's docs say that private use goes through this custom connection. Only a public listing can be opened from a direct link. On 2026-10-01, OpenAI staff said that "all eligible users" can now add custom servers without a separate developer-mode toggle. Reports up to 2026-10-07 show that some accounts, including Free, still need the toggle. Work and school workspaces need an admin. Before 2026-10-10, nothing shows a share link or unlisted listing that reaches another personal account. Workspace sharing stays inside the workspace.

Published plugins reach Free, Go, Plus and Pro. Go is less certain, because the docs only imply it. Business turns plugins on by default; Enterprise and Edu turn them off by default. Plugins run on web, desktop, iOS and Android. Teens aged 13 to 17 are part of the expected audience.

I could not confirm availability in the EU/EEA, the UK or Switzerland. The help center, openai.com and chatgpt.com all returned 403. The current plugin docs list no regional exclusion. A public listing costs no fee in the docs, but community reports give review times of 1 to 4 months.

Effect on the plan: until King Down is listed, most invited friends must play in the browser. "Play in ChatGPT" on the join page becomes real only after a public listing.

Result: the D4 reason in 02 §2.6, "no product we checked corrects for faction choice", is only partly true. No product I checked adds a fitted term for each faction to one shared rating when players choose their faction. But products do correct for asymmetry in other ways:
- Two products keep one rating for each faction: StarCraft II (one 1v1 MMR for each race) and Gwent (one MMR for each faction, and the overall rating is the average of the best 4).
- Two products fit an advantage term from data. Since November 2025, Lichess gives White 12 Elo in standard chess and 20 in Crazyhouse, and 0 in Chess960 and Horde. OGS converts handicap stones and komi into an effective rank difference.
- Two products use bids. Twilight Struggle has 'Bid for Sides' in influence. Scythe auctions each faction and mat pair for coins that come off the final score, and it bans 2 combinations.
- Netrunner makes players play both sides: double-sided Swiss, or single-sided Swiss that tracks each player's side bias.
- Polytopia changes the tribes' rules (extra starting stars).

The most common pattern (about 9 of 14) is one rating for each mode or variant, with no faction term. Players choose or get factions freely, and the rating ignores them. Examples are Hearthstone, Battlegrounds, BGA, the pychess asymmetric-army variants, Lichess Chess960 and Horde, Twilight Struggle digital (community report), Dune: Imperium ranked, Root and Polytopia. No product I checked publishes official win rates for each faction. jinteki.net shows each player's Corp and Runner record, and Root and Dune players ask for these numbers.

Size of the effect: the King Down power spread of 42-57% (engine games) is about -56 to +49 Elo. This is about 4 times the Lichess colour term. Engine numbers overstate human edges.

Inference for D4: when both players choose freely from the same list of powers, a strong power is a balance problem, not a rating-fairness problem. So a separate 'Powers' rating pool with no term is the common, low-cost pattern, if the rules keep the choice symmetric. Recommendations:
- Correct the D4 wording.
- Record each player's power in every rated game.
- Publish win rates for each power.
- Give powers a separate rated pool. Do not make one rating for each power.
- Keep a per-power term at 0 until enough human data exists, and rebalance the rules first.
- Keep the printed (2017) and lab readings out of rated play.

Supabase publishes one benchmark for Broadcast from Database. It shows a median of 46 ms, p95 of 132 ms and p99 of 159 ms, at 80,000 users and 10,000 inserts per second. The test used realtime.broadcast_changes, which writes to the same table and WAL path as realtime.send. Supabase added this benchmark to its docs on 2025-03-26. The load generators ran inside AWS, so the numbers do not include the player's own internet connection. Client-to-client Broadcast is faster: p95 28 to 49 ms in the normal tests. ## Gap 6 (cross): When King Down adds handles, public profiles, preset messages and multiplayer, what do the UK Online Safety Act and the EU Digital Services Act require from a very small game service that has UK and EU users? For the UK, check Ofcom's codes for user-to-user services: illegal-content risk assessment, children's access assessment and age checks. For the EU, check the exemptions for micro and small enterprises. Do preset-only messages and usernames make a game a 'user-to-user service'? [`gap6`]

The UK Online Safety Act (OSA) almost certainly applies to King Down once players can play each other and see each other's handles, if the game has a "significant number" of UK users or targets the UK. The OSA has no size exemption. A user-to-user service is any service where content that one user makes can reach another user. Content includes "data of any description", so game moves count, and the share of user content does not matter. Handles count as "identifying content". The Act exempts identifying content only when it goes with email, SMS or comments on the provider's own content, and a game has none of these. Ofcom names matchmaking, player profiles and moving objects in a game as user-to-user features. No source says that preset messages take a game out of scope; the Act even treats "applying an emoji" as a way for users to communicate. So presets do not remove the duties. They do lower the risk level, and the risk level decides how many code measures apply.

Every in-scope service, of any size, must do and record the following:
- an illegal content risk assessment that covers 18 kinds of priority illegal content (guidance v2.0, 25 June 2026);
- a children's access assessment;
- probably a children's risk assessment, because only highly effective age checks let a service say "children cannot use it". ChatGPT plugins must suit ages 13 to 17, and Ofcom lists games as content that attracts children.

Code measures for all services (codes dated 9 September 2026):
- a named accountable person;
- a moderation function that reviews content and takes it down fast;
- a reporting and complaints system that is easy to use, with appeals;
- terms of service that say how users are protected, written for the youngest permitted user.

Since 7 April 2026, providers must also report detected child sexual abuse content to the National Crime Agency. A chess game that bans the worst content and can remove it does not need age checks. Ofcom fined 4chan £20,000 for not answering an information notice. It then fined 4chan £50,000 for a missing risk assessment and £20,000 for its terms of service. In April 2026 Ofcom also opened investigations into two small chat sites.

EU Digital Services Act (DSA): it applies only if King Down "offers services in the Union". That needs an EU establishment, a significant number of EU users compared with a country's population, or targeting of EU countries. A website that people in the EU can simply open is not enough. If the DSA applies, King Down is a hosting service and maybe an online platform. Micro and small firms do not have to follow Articles 19 to 28, which include Article 28 on minors, or publish transparency reports. Articles 11 to 18 still apply: contact points, an EU legal representative if King Down has no EU establishment, terms content, a notice form, statements of reasons, and alerts to the police about threats to life.
